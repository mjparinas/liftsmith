# Public pages and SEO (owner request, 2026-11-09)

Part of the decision record. [AGENTS.md](../../AGENTS.md) has the rules that apply to every change and the list of topics. Record decisions about this topic here, in the same change as the code.

- Like Gradelings: statically generated pages for every exercise, muscle, piece of equipment, muscle-and-equipment pair (only pairs with 3+ exercises), exercise alternatives, tools (plate calculator, one-rep max calculator) and training guides (RIR, deloads, progressive overload). All are in `sitemap.xml` with structured data (`HowTo`, `ItemList`, `WebApplication`, `Article`, `FAQPage`), and each ends with a call to action into the app. Built from the same data as the app.
- The app (`/app/`) is `noindex` and disallowed in `robots.txt`.
- **Built (2026-11-09):** `src/lib/seo.ts` is the one route list. `siteRoutes()` feeds `src/app/sitemap.ts` (`/sitemap.xml`, 310 URLs at launch, every one on the www host), so a new exercise, muscle pairing or article gets its page and its sitemap entry with no other change. Pages: `/exercises/` and `/exercises/<id>/` (setup and cues as `HowTo` data, muscles, equipment and the app's own 3D demo with its controls), `/exercises/<id>/alternatives/` (the app's swap ranking with no gym limit), `/muscles/` and `/muscles/<muscle>/`, `/muscles/<muscle>/<equipment>/` (only pairs with 3+ exercises, `PAIR_MIN`), `/equipment/` and `/equipment/<item>/` (only items with exercises), `/guides/` (reps in reserve, deload week, progressive overload), `/blog/`, and `/tools/` with the plate calculator and one-rep max calculator (the app's `plates.ts` and `onerm.ts`, bump buttons and chips, nothing stored) beside the TDEE calculator. Slugs are exercise ids and kebab-cased muscle and equipment ids. The site is English only; `MUSCLE_NAME` and `EQUIPMENT_NAME` in `seo.ts` are its labels.
- **Blog** (Melvin, 2026-11-09: "many blog pages with CTA on it that redirect it to our apps, emphasis on the call to actions"). Guides and posts are data in `src/content/` (`articles.ts` for the types and call-to-action copy, `guides.ts`, `blog/*.ts`; 20 posts at launch) rendered by `ArticleView.tsx`, with `Article`/`BlogPosting` and `FAQPage` data. Inline text takes `[links](/path/)` and `**bold**`. Rules, checked by `seo.test.ts`: original writing; only features that ship; no other apps' or trademarked programs' names; every link goes to a page that exists; every article has a call to action in its body after the intro. Add a post by adding an entry; it appears on `/blog/`, in related reading and in the sitemap. **More posts (Melvin, 2026-11-10: "more blog posts to attract more search users"):** 18 more in `blog/d.ts` and `blog/e.ts` (38 in all), aimed at high-intent searches the site didn't cover: push pull legs, upper/lower, the 5×5 workout, days a week, rep ranges, linear vs double progression, compound vs isolation, raising the bench, squat and deadlift, a budget home gym, kettlebell workouts, calisthenics progressions, women starting out, lifting over 40, HRV and readiness, starting weights and body recomposition. Each links to exercise, muscle, equipment, guide and tool pages, ends on the app feature that answers it, and most carry `faq` entries for `FAQPage` data. Training claims are general guidance with the study named where one is cited; Pro features are labelled Pro. **Posts on new features (Melvin, 2026-11-11: "based on all these new features … more blog posts for seo"):** 7 more in `blog/f.ts` (45 in all), each answering a search the new features answer: velocity-based training with the phone camera (camera bar speed lab), camera rep counting (Labs), lifting with a smartwatch (Wear OS, Apple Watch, and Garmin as on its way until its store listing exists), drop sets and rest-pause, training to failure, taking body measurements, and Health Connect and Apple Health for lifters. Four new call-to-action topics go with them (`labs`, `watch`, `measure`, `health`). Strava, Oura and WHOOP aren't named as direct connections while they show Coming soon; Oura and Garmin are named as reaching us through the health store. Then 5 in `blog/g.ts` (50 in all) on the 300-exercise library: a resistance band workout, cable machine exercises, machines vs free weights, cardio with strength training (time and distance logging) and conditioning finishers. Then 4 in `blog/h.ts` (54 in all) on stretching: stretches after lifting, dynamic vs static stretching, a rest-day routine and tight hips, with a `stretch` call to action. Stretches have no public pages (they aren't exercises), so posts name them as the app does ("Lunge with a reach", "Lying twist") instead of linking.
- **One header on every public page** (Melvin, 2026-11-11: "the navigation options goes from Features, 3D demos, Pricing, FAQ, Blog to Exercises, Guides, Blog, Tools, Pricing … seems wrong"). The home page used to draw its own header with its own links. Now every public page, the home page, `/privacy/` and `/delete-account/` included, uses `SiteHeader` (`SiteChrome.tsx`) with one link list, `NAV`: Features, Exercises, Guides, Blog, Tools, Pricing, FAQ. The home page's sections are `/#features` and `/#faq`, so they work from any page; Pricing is the `/pricing/` page everywhere. Wide screens (lg+) show every link inline; narrower screens keep the logo, Sign in and Start free, with the links in a menu (`SiteMenu.tsx`, a `<details>` so it opens without script; it closes on a link, Escape or a tap outside). It fits a 320 px phone without sideways scroll. `siteNav.test.tsx` checks the links match on the home page, the blog and the plain pages.
- **Calls to action on every public page** (`SiteChrome.tsx`: `SitePage`, `CtaButton`, `CtaInline`, `CtaBanner`, `StickyCta`): the header's Start free, a prompt near the top, one or more inline prompts written for the page's topic, an end banner with the store badges, and a bar pinned to the bottom on phones. Every placement has its own `data-cta` (`header`, `blog-top`, `blog-mid`, `blog-mid-2`, `blog-end`, `exercise-mid`, `sticky-<kind>`, ...), which the site beacon records with the path, so the admin panel's Traffic tab shows which placements work. They all open `/app/` until the store listings exist; the badges then link to the stores. `seoPages.test.tsx` checks every article and page type has its calls to action. The home page uses the same footer, with links to every section.

Every call to action into the app carries its own `data-cta` name so the site beacon counts each placement. All names come from `BRAND`, so a rename needs no page edits.

- **Comparison pages** (`/compare/fitbod/`, `/compare/hevy/`, `/compare/strong/`, `/compare/gymverse/`, `/alternatives/fitbod/`): every competitor fact lives in one data file with its source URL and the date checked, and the build fails when any row is more than 90 days old. Competitors are named in text only (no logos or screenshots), with a trademark line. Claims are factual and dated, never disparaging. **Built (2026-11-09):** `/compare/` (index), `/compare/fitbod/`, `/compare/hevy/`, `/compare/strong/`, `/compare/gymverse/` and `/alternatives/fitbod/`. The data file is `src/content/compare.ts`: each competitor's facts by topic (price, free plan, planning, recovery, blocks, plates, rest timer, social, platforms, health apps, data, ads), each with its source URL and check date, beside `OURS` (what Liftsmith does, only features that ship). Sources are the apps' own sites and US store listings (the pricing pages refused automated reads); where a listing shows several prices, the page says exactly what it shows rather than picking one. Topics a competitor's pages don't cover are left out, never guessed. Each page also says when the other app may suit the reader better. `seo.test.ts` fails when a fact is over 90 days old (re-check the source, then update the text and the date) and on harsh words; the pages carry the usual calls to action and the trademark line.
- **Page speed** (Melvin asked for a PageSpeed Insights review and "execute them all", 2026-11-10). Mobile Lighthouse had the home page at 64 and exercise pages at 65, all from Total Blocking Time (4.9 s and 9.4 s): PageSpeed's test phone has no GPU, so the 3D figure was drawn in software. Text pages were already 98 to 100. The rules now:
  - **3D only on a real GPU.** The hero and the site's demos use `probeWebGL()` (`src/lib/webgl.ts`): software WebGL gets the hero's screenshot, and the exercise page and home page demo get the 2D figure **as a still pose with a Play button** (`autoPlay2d={false}`), since drawing 2D frames is main-thread work too. The app keeps playing 2D on its own.
  - **The hero waits for the page.** three.js is fetched and the figure starts only after `load` and an idle moment (`afterLoad` in `HeroLift.tsx`, at most 2 s), so the text and buttons are ready first.
  - **30 fps and at most 1.5 device pixels** for every demo on the website (`fps`, `maxDpr`; the app keeps 2).
  - **No language catalogues on public pages.** `types.ts` imports `i18n.ts`, which loads every catalogue (all three languages, about 115 KB gzipped). Public client components never import it at run time: `LiftDemoCore` takes `labels`, the pages pass `MUSCLE_NAME`, the hero's script is in `heroProgram.ts` and its plates are worked out on the server (`heroPlates.ts`). The app's `LiftDemo` is `LiftDemoCore` with the translated names. `sitePerf.test.ts` walks the imports and fails if a site component reaches the catalogues again.
  - **Never prefetch the app.** Every `next/link` to `/app/` (and `/me/` in the footer) has `prefetch={false}`; before, every page view downloaded about 350 KB of app code. `sitePerf.test.ts` checks it.
  - **Screenshots at the size shown.** `scripts/site-screenshots.mjs` writes each screenshot full size (780 px) and as a 540 px copy; `Screenshot.tsx` offers both with `srcSet`. Image optimisation stays off (the static export needs it off, and Vercel's is metered).
  - **The app's first screen is measurable:** the `rise` animation starts at opacity 0.01, not 0, because Chrome doesn't count a paint at opacity 0 as the largest contentful paint and PageSpeed reported an error for `/app/`.
  - After the change, local Lighthouse (mobile / desktop): home 90 / 100, exercise page 92 / 100, compare 99 / 100, app 84 / 100, blog 99. Next's own 14 KB of polyfills (`Array.prototype.at` and friends) are built in and stay.

- **Bing and IndexNow** (Melvin, 2026-11-10: "Add Bing"). The site is verified in **Bing Webmaster Tools** by importing it from Google Search Console (no meta tag or file needed; the import also brings the sitemap), so Bing reads the same `/sitemap.xml`. **IndexNow** tells Bing, Yandex, Seznam, Naver and Yep about new and changed pages without waiting for a crawl: the key is `INDEXNOW_KEY` in `src/lib/indexnow.ts` and is served as `public/<key>.txt` (not a secret; IndexNow fetches it from our host to trust the pings). The daily admin job (`/api/cron/admin/`, 13:00 UTC, after the nightly 09:00 promotion) calls `pingIndexNow` (`src/server/indexnow.ts`): every sitemap page that's new, or whose date changed, since the last ping goes to `api.indexnow.org` in one request, and what was sent is kept in `indexnow_sent`, so the first run sends every page once and later runs send only new posts and pages and edited articles. Pages without a date (exercise pages, say) go again only if their address is new; Bing recrawls them from the sitemap. Only production pings (`VERCEL_ENV=production`). A refusal is kept for the next day, and the job's result shows `indexNow: { sent }`. Changing the key means a new file in `public/` and the constant in the same change.
