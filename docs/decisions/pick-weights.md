# Pick weights: how the adaptive plan chooses exercises

- **Each exercise has its own chance of being picked, per lifter** (Melvin, 2026-11-13: "Each exercise should maintain a probability of being picked for adaptive workouts … the most popular ones … bench press (barbell, dumbbell), squats, deadlifts, back rows, shoulder press, military press, bicep curls, isolation curls, pinwheel curls, shrugs, sumo deadlift, lunges … More exotic exercises have a much lower probability. When users like an exercise it should boost the probability, if people swap an exercise it should lower the probability. These are custom to each user"). Code: `src/lib/pickWeights.ts`, used by `pick` in `generator.ts`.
- **A real draw, not a ranking.** Among the exercises that fit a muscle, `generate` now draws one with probability in proportion to its weight (`draw`, from the day's seeded random, so the same day and gym still give the same plan). The weight is the old structural part (big compounds ×3 or ×4 in the first round, bodyweight ×0.5 in a full gym) times the lifter's pick weight. Before this, a seeded score picked the top candidate, and with a thousand exercises most picks were unusual moves (about 10% staples in a full gym; now about 60% staples, 33% common, 7% niche, checked in `pickWeights.test.ts`).
- **Defaults come from popularity:** `STAPLE` (about 65 lifts most people program: the bench presses, squats, deadlifts and sumo, rows, overhead and military presses, curls including concentration and cross-body ones, shrugs, lunges, pulldowns, pull-ups, leg press and the classic machines) start at 1, `COMMON` (about 220 well-known variations) at 0.2, and everything else, including every exercise added later unless it's listed, at 0.012 (`BASE`). The lists are by id and a test checks every id exists. New exercises are niche by default; list one as common or staple only when it really is one.
- **Likes:** the star (`training.favourites`, the existing favourites) is the like. A liked exercise starts as a staple however unusual it is, and is ×40 (`LIKE`), so it's picked on most days its muscle is trained, though no longer every time (its "Favourite" line now says "picked much more often" in all six languages).
- **Swaps:** a plain swap lowers the exercise swapped away (×0.55 each, `SWAP_AWAY`) and raises the one chosen a little (×1.15 each, at most ×1.6). Each swap fades (half after 60 days) and is forgotten after a year, and no exercise drops below 5% of where it started (`FLOOR`), so swaps never bury a lift for good. **"Not available here" swaps don't count**: they say the gym is missing something (a broken machine), not that the lifter dislikes the lift, and they stay a hard filter for two weeks at that gym (`avoided`).
- **Hard filters stay hard.** The weights only choose among exercises that passed equipment, "not available here", injuries (`skippedIds`), recovery and the split day. A like can't bring back anything those took out (tested).
- **Picked less often** (Settings › Workout › Plan details, under Favourite exercises): lists exercises whose swaps brought them below 80% of normal, most lowered first, each with **Reset**, which records the time in `training.pickResets[id]` so earlier swaps of it stop counting. Hidden when empty. Text `train.lessOften*`, `train.pickAgain*` in six languages.
- **Stored and synced without new data:** likes live on the profile and swaps are events, both already synced, so every device works out the same weights and nothing new goes to the server. `pickResets` is on the profile (last write wins with the rest of it).
- **Cached on the device** (Melvin, 2026-11-13: "This is a lot of data so it should be cached locally"). `SwapCache` reads the taste swaps out of the event log once and keeps them in memory and in localStorage (`liftsmith.pickSwaps.v1`, with the event count and the first and last event ids). The next plan reads only the events added since; when the log changed any other way (sync merged older events in, a workout was deleted, an account signed out) it reads it all again. Weights themselves aren't stored: they depend on today's date (swaps fade) and are quick to work out from the swaps.
- **Why lines:** a liked pick keeps its "and a favourite" reason on Today (`why.favourite`). A popular pick needs no extra reason; the muscle reason already says why that muscle was trained.
- **Platforms:** pure web code in the shared engine, so Android, iOS and the web app get it together. Mutation-tested with the engine (`stryker.config.mjs`) and in the stricter engine type check.
