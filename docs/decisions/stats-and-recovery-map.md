# Stats and recovery map

Part of the decision record. [AGENTS.md](../../AGENTS.md) has the rules that apply to every change and the list of topics. Record decisions about this topic here, in the same change as the code.

- **The recovery map is the exercise demos' 3D figure** (Melvin, 2026-11-09: "use the same 3d models with the muscles highlighted, fall back to 2d if the device doesn't support it"). `RecoveryMap.tsx` (Recovery screen and Today's "Why these muscles") shows the standing hologram figure (the body from Settings' sex, male when unset) with every muscle drawn **inside a glass body as a glowing volume** in its recovery band's colour (Melvin picked this "glow" style from four samples, 2026-11-09), Front / Back buttons, and drag to turn. It uses the demo engine (`engine.ts` `paint`, `hatch`, `selected`, `calm`; `scene3d.ts`), with these changes for a map: each muscle's patch of skin is pulled in toward its limb's core with a belly shape, and triangles that cross between two muscles are left out so each muscle stands apart; a hidden depth-only core inside the muscles so the far side never shows through; the skin turns to a faint neutral wireframe (so Fresh green and the colour-blind blue can't be mistaken for the hologram's cyan); and the band colours taken from the **dark** theme's tokens (`STAGE_BANDS` in `colours.ts`, kept equal to `globals.css` by `a11y.test.ts`) because the stage is always dark; Settings › Accessibility › Colour-blind safe switches it to the `-cvd` set. **Fatigued muscles also get stripes**, and the legend shows that mark, so colour is never the only cue; the canvas's label lists every band's muscles in words. Turns snap instead of easing in Lean mode or with reduced motion, and nothing else moves. Same WebGL check as the demos (`detectMode`); **without WebGL (or if the 3D scene fails) it falls back to the 2D map** (`src/components/BodyMap.tsx`, front and back with dots and stripes).
- **Tap a muscle to see what to do** (action over stats; `src/lib/muscleAdvice.ts`): on the figure (the engine picks the painted muscle nearest the camera under the finger), in the Recovery screen's muscle list, or in the band list under the map. It says the call and why from the same model the generator plans with: rest it or go light, with when it's back to Ready (70%, found by searching the recovery curve), or how many sets are left of this week's goal, or that the week's sets are done; plus when it was last worked. A recovered muscle with half or more of its weekly sets left offers **Make it a priority** (`togglePriority`). The 3D map, picking and advice are pure web code, so Android, iOS and the web get them together. The recovery map is Pro (see Money); the billing work adds the gate.
- **A picked muscle is obvious** (Melvin, 2026-11-11: "the highlighting isn't obvious especially with smaller muscles, can you bump up the contrast"). On the 3D figure (`picked` in the engine's settings, set only for a tapped muscle) the picked muscle glows solid and swells up to the skin, the skin over it lights up in its colour like an outline, and every other muscle fades to a dim grey with the rest of the skin dimmed too, so it's the only colour on the figure. Today's focus muscles keep the softer lit-and-dimmed look, because there the other muscles' colours still matter. Picking calves turns the figure to the back, where they show. The 2D map outlines the picked muscle (3 px ink stroke) and fades the others (and their marks) back. The picked row in the Recovery list gets an accent wash and a bar down its leading edge (`.picked-row`), and its name goes bold; picked chips get a thicker accent border. The canvas stage is always dark, so the figure looks the same in light mode; the row and chips use theme tokens. No pulse: the map draws only on change (battery). Shared web code, so Android, iOS and the web get it together.
- Stats: weekly sets per muscle against the target, estimated one-rep max trends, PRs, volume, session history, streaks and trophies.
- **History is a month calendar** (Melvin, 2026-11-11: "a calendar view of past workouts somewhere where we can review what we did"; `HistoryCalendar` in `History.tsx`, `src/lib/history.ts`). It replaced the plain, untappable list of the last 20 workouts at the bottom of Progress (same "History" section, so Progress got shorter). A month grid from the lifter's first day of the week (`monthGrid` from `dashboard.ts`, shared with the web dashboard's Calendar tab); each workout day is a filled tile with up to four dots for the muscle families its sets trained (push blue, pull cyan, legs violet, core ember, the `theme.ts` family hues) and a small trophy on a PR day, with a legend in words under the grid; today is ringed. Only workout days are buttons, and each one's label reads the date, sets, PRs and families, so colour is never the only cue. Change month with ‹ › or a sideways swipe on the calendar (48 px; touches within 24 px of an edge are left to the phone's back gesture), from the first workout's month to this one. It opens on the month's latest workout day, and the picked day's workouts show in full under the grid: time, length, sets, volume, average heart rate and PRs, then each exercise with every set (`setText`, so dumbbells, holds and cardio read right) and a trophy on the set that made a PR (`prSetIds`). **The workout's name** sits under its date (Melvin, 2026-11-11, asking what a 5×5 or a push/pull day looks like): the saved routine's name, or the program and its day ("Classic 5×5 · Workout A", "Push/pull/legs · Pull day"), from what the `sessionStart` event recorded (`workoutName` in `history.ts`; each program engine gives its day names through `dayName`, `programDayLabel` in `programs.ts`). Adaptive workouts have no name, and a removed routine or program just drops it. The dots stay what was actually trained, whatever the plan was called, so a 5×5 day shows legs, push and pull and a bodyweight day shows whatever its moves worked. Reviewing what was done is the point here, so it isn't a coach's note; the notes at the top of Progress stay the "what to do next". Pure web code, so Android, iOS and the web app get it together.
- **One-rep max calculator** (Melvin, 2026-11-09: "use the latest research and algorithms, use as much existing data as possible"; `src/lib/onerm.ts`, `OneRepMax.tsx`). Every exercise with data is on Progress; tapping one opens its estimate, likely range and confidence, the set it leans on, a reps-to-weight table and a "try a set" calculator with every formula's answer.
  - Reps to failure = reps + RIR. The RIR is the lifter's reported effort, else 0 for AMRAP, challenge and missed sets, else the plan's RIR less any extra reps, else 2 for web-logged and imported sets (counted for less).
  - Formula by rep range: up to 10 reps to failure, the mean of Brzycki, Epley and Marzagao's 2026 weight-dependent equation (arXiv 2603.17495, fitted on 303,494 near-failure sets); above 10, Mayhew, Wathan and Marzagao. Sets past 20 reps to failure are ignored; high-rep, far-from-failure and guessed-RIR sets count for less.
  - Each session's best set is one point. Today's max is the quality- and recency-weighted mean (6-week half-life) of the best three points from the last 6 months, after a detraining mark-down (none for 3 weeks, then about 1% a month). The setup max is used only until a lift has logged sets.
  - The strength score and strength twins use the same estimate (`strength.ts` calls `oneRm`), so the cards on Progress agree. PRs and XP stay on plain Epley (`derive.ts`, `game.ts`) because they are recomputed from all history and a new formula would quietly add or remove earned PRs, XP and badges (Melvin agreed, 2026-11-09).
- **DOTS score** (Melvin, 2026-11-09) from the estimated back squat, bench press and deadlift maxes and the latest weigh-in, with the published DOTS coefficients (men's or women's). When the profile has a sex set (setup or Settings), the card uses it and hides the formula picker; the Men's / Women's chips show only when sex isn't set (Melvin, 2026-11-09). Needs all three lifts; the lifter can bump any number with steppers to see "what if". Rough bands: 200 novice, 300 intermediate, 400 advanced, 500 elite.
- **Calculators take no typing** (Melvin, 2026-11-09): steppers with − and + buttons, chips for reps in reserve, a picker for the exercise.
- **Volume reads as a plain number under 1,000** (Melvin, 2026-11-11: "0k kg" with no workouts is a bug). `fmtVolume` in `units.ts` is the one formatter for total lifted on Progress and the web dashboard: "0 kg", "640 lb", then thousands with one decimal from the `progress.volumeK` line in each language ("12.4k kg", "12,4 Tsd. kg"). The dashboard's tonnes ("t") and hard-coded "k lb" went with it, so kg and lb read the same way everywhere.
