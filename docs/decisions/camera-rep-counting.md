# Camera rep counting (Melvin asked, 2026-11-09: "rep counter by camera or watch motion", then "use the camera for workouts… a toggle during that exercise to auto count", after trying the lab)

Part of the decision record. [AGENTS.md](../../AGENTS.md) has the rules that apply to every change and the list of topics. Record decisions about this topic here, in the same change as the code.

- **On the workout screen, for exercises the camera can read** (`profileFor` in `profiles.ts` has a profile; holds, carries, shrugs and wrist curls don't). A **Count reps** button at the bottom left of the exercise demo turns it on, and **Stop counting** on the counting stage turns it off (`AutoCount.tsx`). It's optional and never adds a tap during a set: the reps counted fill in the reps box, so Log set stays one tap, and the lifter can still bump the number. Each logged set (or warm-up) starts a fresh count; nothing is counted during rest. If the tracker ends a set on its own after 8 s still, the next reps add to the same count.
- **While it's on, the 3D figure follows the lifter** (`LiftDemo follow`, side view) with the live count in the corner. **The camera sits beside the figure** (Melvin, 2026-11-10: "shift the 3D model left"): the figure takes the left of the stage and the camera preview the right 42%, full height, mirrored for the front camera, with a dot that turns green when the whole body is in view. **The bones the camera reads are drawn over the lifter** on the preview (Melvin, 2026-11-10: "bones drawn on the camera superimposed over my image"): accent-coloured bones and mint joints from the pose model's landmarks (`src/lib/reps/skeleton.ts`: `BONES`, and `coverPoint` to match the preview's `object-fit: cover` crop), leaving out any point the model is under 50% sure of. Drawn on a canvas in memory each frame; nothing is recorded. **Front camera by default**, with a flip button on the preview. On or off, the camera side and the source are remembered on the device (localStorage `liftsmith.autoCount`), so it stays on across exercises and workouts until switched off.
- **A lab** (Melvin, 2026-11-10: "put this behind a feature flag" in a Pro-only Labs section): Count reps shows on the workout screen only while **Settings › Labs › Camera rep counting** is on (the `labRepCount` flag, off by default, `labOn` in `src/lib/labs.ts`). Labs is Pro, so without Pro it's off whatever the flag says, and free lifters see the Labs row on Settings marked Pro (it opens the Pro screen). This replaced the `autoCount` Pro feature and PR #169's Pro-tagged Count reps button. The lab screen of 2026-11-09 (`#/lab`, the `repLab` flag) was removed once counting reached the workout screen.
- **Phone motion** counting was unreliable in Melvin's tests, so it's a second lab, **Phone motion rep counting** (the `repMotion` flag, off), with its own switch in Labs. **It works on its own** (Melvin, 2026-11-11: "Phone motion rep counting should not depend on camera rep counting"): with only phone motion on, Count reps on the workout screen counts with the phone's motion sensors (labelled "Count reps with phone motion", with a phone icon); with only camera counting on, it uses the camera; with both on, the stage offers "Use phone motion" and "Use the camera" and remembers the choice (`countSource` in `AutoCount.tsx`). (The five quick taps on the Settings title that used to switch it were removed: Labs is the one place for it.)
- **Camera bar speed** (Melvin, 2026-11-11: "Camera bar speed", in place of the bar speed sensors on Connected apps' old "Coming later" list) is a third lab (`labBarSpeed`, off, needs camera counting). While counting, the badge under the rep count shows the last full rep's mean lifting speed in m/s (the lifter's height from Your stats gives the camera its scale; without it, the share of the set's fastest rep), and the status line turns it into an action: from the third full rep, "about N reps left in the tank" (reps in reserve from velocity loss, `rirFromVelocityLoss`), and at 30% loss or more (`STOP_LOSS`, about one rep left) "a good place to end the set", in amber with one warning buzz. The numbers come from the rep tracker's own summary (`barSpeed` in `src/lib/reps/barSpeed.ts`); the `barSpeed` glossary "i" beside the status line explains it. Works with phone motion too. Nothing is saved yet: it's advice for the set in front of the lifter.
- **Camera form check** (Melvin, 2026-11-11, Labs idea 3: "flag squat depth, short ROM and lateral lean, give a short tip between sets, and show the issue on the 3D figure") is a fourth lab (`labFormCheck`, off, needs camera counting; camera only, not phone motion). While counting, each frame's landmarks also give two form signals (`formFrame` in `src/lib/reps/form.ts`): **depth**, the hip's height against the knee in thigh lengths (from the better-seen leg), and **lean**, the torso's tilt to one side from upright, read only when the lifter faces the camera (both shoulders and hips seen, shoulders at least 45% of the torso's length apart), signed towards the lifter's own right whichever way the image is mirrored. When a set is logged, `assessForm` judges its full and partial reps (failed attempts aren't judged): **depth** on squats only (a rep whose hip never gets within 0.08 thigh lengths of the knee stopped above parallel; the hip landmark is the joint, a little above the crease parallel is judged by), **short range** on every lift (the reps the tracker already grades partial), and **lean** on squats, hinges and overhead presses (7° or more through the bottom half of the rep, the same way on most of them; a wobble both ways isn't a lean). An issue needs a quarter of the reps (and at least one) before it's mentioned, so the check leans towards saying nothing. `mainIssue` picks the one tip: the issue on the biggest share of reps, a lean first on a tie, then depth, then range; on squats depth stands in for short range. **During rest the status line carries that one tip** with its numbers ("3 of 5 reps stopped above parallel. Sit until your hips are level with your knees, and lighten the bar if you need to.", or that nothing needed fixing), in amber, with the `formCheck` glossary "i"; the next set clears it. **The figure shows it:** for depth or short range it replays the lifter's short rep, down to where those reps stopped and back every 2.4 s (held at that point in Lean mode); for a lean it turns to face the screen and tips 8° to 15° the way the lifter leaned (`lean` on `LiftDemo`, added to the figure's roll, so the 2D fallback tips too). Nothing is added during a set and nothing is saved. Pure web code, so Android, iOS and the web app get it together. Thresholds are first guesses from simulated poses (`form.test.ts`); real recordings should tune them before it leaves Labs.
- **Today's max from warm-ups** (Melvin, 2026-11-11, Labs idea 2: "estimate today's max from warm-up bar speeds and nudge working weight before the first hard set") is a fifth lab (`labSpeedMax`, off, needs camera bar speed). While the camera counts a warm-up, its fastest full rep in m/s is kept with its weight (so it needs the lifter's height); an undone warm-up drops its speed, and nothing is saved. In the rest after the last warm-up, a card fits the line speed = a + b·weight through the warm-ups (the load-velocity profile) and reads off the weight at the lift's minimum velocity threshold (`MVT`: squat and lunge 0.30 m/s, hinge 0.15, bench-type press 0.17, overhead press 0.19, row 0.50, pull-up 0.23; other patterns get nothing). That's today's estimated max, compared with the max the plan expects (Epley from the working weight, reps and RIR). Within 5% the card says the plan stands; otherwise it says how far off and why, and offers the working weight one tap away ("Use 105 kg"), at most 5% heavier or 10% lighter (a slow day is the one to be careful on), rounded to a weight the gym can make; if the gym's steps are too coarse for that, the plan stands. It stays quiet with under two warm-up weights, a line that doesn't slope down, three or more warm-ups that fit it badly (r² under 0.6), or a heaviest warm-up under half the planned max. Straight sets keep the new weight; a program's per-set ramp is left alone. The tap goes through `adjust`, so the notification, watches and Live Activity follow. Code: `speedMax` in `src/lib/reps/speedMax.ts`, the card in `workout/SpeedNudge.tsx`, the `speedMax` glossary "i" on it. Pure web code, so Android, iOS and the web get it together. Camera speeds are a body point scaled by height, not the bar itself, so absolute thresholds may sit off for some lifters; a per-lifter calibration from their own past warm-ups is the next step if the estimates run high or low.
- **Bar path replay** (Melvin, 2026-11-11, Labs idea 4: "after a camera-counted set, a short replay on the device draws the bar's path from the pose landmarks, with one cue") is a sixth camera lab (`labBarPath`, off, needs camera counting; camera only). On squats, hinges and overhead presses (`pathFits`), each counted frame also gives the bar point (the shoulders on a squat, where the bar sits; the hands on the others), from the better-seen side, and the mid-foot (between heel and toe, or the ankle), with the way the lifter faces from toe against heel (or nose against ear), so forward means forward whichever way the image is mirrored. A rep's drift is the bar's largest move forward or back from where that rep started, smoothed over five frames and turned into centimetres from the leg length the camera sees and the lifter's height (1.70 m when Your stats has none). When a set is logged, `assessPath` (`src/lib/reps/barPath.ts`) judges full and partial reps: a drift of 3.5% of height (about 6 cm) or more counts, split into **forward at the bottom** (peaking up to a quarter of the rep past the turnaround), **forward on the way up** and **back**; like form check, it needs a quarter of the reps (and at least one) before it says anything, and the most common drift wins. **During rest** the camera panel shows the replay instead of the live preview (`BarPathReplay.tsx`): the worst rep with that drift (or the deepest rep when there's none) as a faint stick figure from its own landmarks, the bar's path drawn in the accent as it plays, a mint dot on the bar and a dashed line straight up from where it started, looping with a short hold; Lean mode and reduce motion draw the whole path once, still. The status line carries one cue with its numbers ("The bar drifted about 8 cm forward at the bottom on 3 of 5 reps. Keep it over the middle of your foot…", or that it stayed within N cm), with the `barPath` glossary "i". With form check on too, a form issue goes first, then a bar path drift, then whichever says all is well. The next set clears it. **The clip stays on the device:** only landmarks are kept, in memory, for at most about a minute of frames, until the next set or the screen closes; no video is recorded and nothing is saved. Pure web code, so Android, iOS and the web app get it together. The thresholds are first guesses from simulated poses (`barPath.test.ts`); real side-on recordings should tune them before it leaves Labs.
- Nothing about counting is saved yet beyond the reps the lifter logs.
- **One rep engine for every source** (`src/lib/reps/tracker.ts`): follows the excursion from the rest position (5-sample median, then a One Euro filter), counts an attempt each time the lifter leaves rest and comes back, and grades it when the set ends: **full**, **partial** (under 80% of the set's deepest rep or under the profile's minimum range), **failed** (didn't come back within 6 s, tracking lost for 1.5 s mid-rep, or sank back to the bottom after getting partway up). **Grinders** are full reps 50%+ slower than the set's fastest, or with a stall (under 15% of the rep's peak speed for 250 ms+). **Set length** runs from the first movement to the end of the last attempt; the set ends itself after 8 s idle. **Bar speed** is mean concentric velocity: m/s when the scale is known (camera: lifter's height and leg or arm length; motion: integrated), otherwise degrees a second. **Reps in reserve** is a rough estimate from velocity loss (after Sánchez-Medina and González-Badillo 2011).
- **Camera:** MediaPipe Pose Landmarker Lite (33 points, Apache 2.0) in the web view, GPU delegate with a CPU fallback, loaded on first use from **our own copies** (2026-11-10, security audit): `scripts/mediapipe.mjs` copies the runtime from `node_modules` (pinned by the lockfile) into `public/mediapipe/tasks-vision-<version>/` before every dev, build and static build, and the model is committed as `public/mediapipe/pose_landmarker_lite-f16-v1.task` with its SHA-256 checked in `mediapipe.test.ts`. The apps carry both inside (about 20 MB, so counting works offline); the website serves them from its own origin and the service worker caches them. No CDN. Joint angles per exercise come from `profiles.ts` (knee for squats, hip for hinges, elbow for presses, rows and curls, shoulder for raises); a side view is the setup we ask for. Frames are read in memory and dropped: **video never leaves the device and is never recorded.**
- **Motion:** `imu.ts` prefers the platform's fused data (vertical linear acceleration plus rotation rate; DeviceMotionEvent now, CMDeviceMotion on watchOS and Wear OS linear-acceleration sensors later) and falls back to raw |a| - g. Zero-velocity resets at still moments and a snap back to rest at lockout keep double-integration drift from building up. Lifts where the hands don't move (pull-ups, dips, push-ups) get no motion profile.
- **Accuracy is measured on simulated sets** (`simulate.ts`, `evaluate.ts`; pass marks in `reps.test.ts`; full report with `REP_ACCURACY=1 npx vitest run src/lib/reps`). Real recordings with hand-counted truth must replace them before this ships.
