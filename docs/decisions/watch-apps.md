# Watch apps (Wear OS and Apple Watch)

Part of the decision record. [AGENTS.md](../../AGENTS.md) has the rules that apply to every change and the list of topics. Record decisions about this topic here, in the same change as the code.

Melvin approved building them on 2026-11-10 ("Yes to all", after the list of what was still missing).

## What the watch does

- **The phone's workout, on the wrist.** The watch shows exactly what the live notification and the Live Activity show, from the same `WorkoutSession.update` call (`src/lib/sessionNotify.ts`): the set ("Set 2 of 4"), the exercise, the weight and reps, the plates, or during rest a big countdown with the next set under it, plus heart rate. Two buttons: **Log set** (always) and **+30 s** (during rest). Nothing else: no planning, no settings, no history. The phone stays the one place where a workout is planned and logged, so there's no second copy of the engine and nothing to merge.
- **The set timer on the wrist** (2026-11-12): while a hold, carry or cardio timer runs, all three watches show its clock with what it's counting ("Time left", "Switch sides") and buzz as each phase ends, counted on the watch's own clock from the phases the phone sends ([Set timer](set-timer.md)).
- **Taps are the notification's buttons.** Log set and +30 s from the watch reach the web app as the same `action` events the notification and Live Activity buttons send (`onSessionAction` in `App.tsx`), so they log the set as planned and start rest the same way. The phone then sends the new state back. The phone only accepts `log` and `add30` from a watch (`WatchMessages` on both platforms).
- **Heart rate from the watch's own sensor.** While a workout runs, the watch reads heart rate and sends it to the phone at most every 2 s, where it arrives as a `heartRate` event (`onWatchHeartRate`). A Bluetooth monitor (a strap, or a watch broadcasting its heart rate) still wins when one is live; otherwise a watch reading counts while it's under 10 s old (`pickBpm` in `src/lib/watchHr.ts`). Watch readings go into the workout's heart rate log only while no monitor is live, so one workout never mixes two streams. The finished workout's average and maximum, and the write to Health Connect or Apple Health, then work exactly as with a strap.
- **A buzz when rest ends**, on the wrist, if the lifter's rest-over alert vibrates (Workout preferences). It follows the same `vibrate` flag the phone uses.
- **Opens itself when a workout starts.** The first update of a new workout (`sessionId`) opens the watch app once: on Apple Watch through `HKHealthStore.startWatchApp` (the system's own way for a phone app to start a watch workout), on Wear OS through `RemoteActivityHelper` to watches that have our app (the `liftsmith_watch` capability). After that the lifter can leave it; the watch keeps up in the background.
- **When the workout ends** (End, Discard or the automatic end of a forgotten workout, which all call `stopSession`), the phone clears the watch's state and the watch goes back to "No workout running · Start one on your phone and it shows here." A state the phone stopped updating more than 3 hours ago is treated as ended, the same limit as the phone's auto-end.
- **No workout is saved from the watch.** The phone already writes the finished workout to Health Connect or Apple Health, so the Apple Watch's workout session is discarded at the end rather than saved twice.
- **Starting a workout from the watch** isn't offered: the phone app has to be open for its plan, and neither platform lets a watch open a phone app in the foreground. Voice and system shortcuts on the phone cover a hands-free start.

## How it's built

- **The message**: one JSON object with the notification's fields (`title`, `setLabel`, `next`, `plates`, `restUntil`, `hr`, `vibrate`, `done`, `total`, `sessionId`) plus `at` (when it was sent). Same keys on both platforms.
- **Wear OS** (`android/wear`, a second Gradle module, Java and plain views like the rest of the native code; Wear OS 3+, API 30). Same application id (`com.getliftsmith.app`) and signing key as the phone app, which the Data Layer needs. The phone's `WatchLink.java` puts the state in one Data Layer item, `/liftsmith/workout`, which the system keeps in step and delivers to a watch that was asleep or out of range as soon as it's back; ending deletes it. The watch's `PhoneListenerService` hands changes to `WatchHub`, the watch's single source of truth, which the screen (`WorkoutActivity`) and `WorkoutService` listen to. Taps and heart rate go back as messages (`/liftsmith/action`, `/liftsmith/hr`) to the phone's `WatchListenerService`, which uses the notification buttons' broadcast. `WorkoutService` is a foreground service of type `health`: it keeps the app alive with the screen off, reads `Sensor.TYPE_HEART_RATE`, and shows the workout as an **Ongoing Activity** (its icon on the watch face). It needs the heart rate permission (`android.permission.health.READ_HEART_RATE` on Wear OS 6, `BODY_SENSORS` before), asked once the first time a workout shows; without it the watch still shows the workout and logs sets.
- **Apple Watch** (`ios/App/LiftsmithWatch`, a single-target SwiftUI watch app embedded in the iPhone app, watchOS 10+, bundle id `com.getliftsmith.app.watchkitapp`). The phone's `WatchLink.swift` sends the state as the WatchConnectivity application context (always the latest, delivered when the watch wakes) and as a message while the watch is reachable. Taps go back as messages, or as queued user info when the phone isn't reachable, so a tap is never lost. `WatchModel` runs an `HKWorkoutSession` (traditional strength training, indoor) while a workout shows: it gives live heart rate and keeps the app running with the wrist down. The watch app has the HealthKit capability and the `workout-processing` background mode.
- **Words** follow the watch's language: English, Spanish, French, German, Italian and Japanese in `android/wear/src/main/res/values*/strings.xml` and `ios/App/LiftsmithWatch/*.lproj/Localizable.strings`. Everything else on screen comes from the phone, already in the app's language.
- **Look**: always dark (the app's dark tokens), the brand blue for the set label, the primary gradient on Log set (Wear OS), the system's prominent button in the brand tint (watchOS), red for heart rate, tabular numbers. The icon is the app's own, copied by `scripts/make-icons.mjs`.
- **Builds**: CI's Android job builds both apps and runs both modules' unit tests (`WorkoutStateTest`, `WatchMessagesTest`) and uploads the Wear APK; the iOS job builds the iPhone app with the watch app embedded. `release.yml` builds the Wear OS bundle with the phone's (the phone's version code is the run number × 2 and the watch's the one after it, because every bundle of one Play app needs its own and Play refuses a code far above the last one; until 2026-11-26 the watch added 100,000,000, which Play blocked) into the release artifact, and uploads it to Play's Wear OS internal track after the phone (`continue-on-error`, so a refusal never stops the phone release). Wear uploads were paused from 2026-11-23 to 2026-11-26 while the Wear OS form factor was out of Play Console. The Apple Watch app goes to TestFlight inside the iPhone app.

## Setup steps in the stores

- **Play Console:** Wear OS has to be added as a form factor once (Test and release › Advanced settings › Form factors › Add Wear OS) before Play takes a Wear bundle. Until then the release's Wear upload step fails on its own (`continue-on-error`) and the phone release goes ahead. Wear OS apps are reviewed against Google's Wear OS quality guidelines, which need watch screenshots in the listing.
- **App Store Connect:** nothing extra. The archive's automatic signing (`-allowProvisioningUpdates`) registers the watch app's bundle id with HealthKit the first time. If it can't, register `com.getliftsmith.app.watchkitapp` with the HealthKit capability in the Apple Developer portal.

## Finding the phone (Play review, 2026-11-20)

Google Play rejected the Wear OS app (version code 100000023): it's marked not standalone, and the reviewer found the watch didn't connect to the phone app as described. The watch only ever said "No workout running · Start one on your phone", with nothing to tap, and it never checked whether the phone had Liftsmith at all. Melvin (2026-11-20): "Fix the wear os but remove the release for now in Google play".

- **It stays not standalone** (`com.google.android.wearable.standalone` false). The watch shows and logs the phone's workout; it has no plan of its own, so calling it standalone would be untrue and fail review the other way.
- **The phone app declares a capability, `liftsmith_phone`** (`android/app/src/main/res/values/wear.xml`), as the watch's `liftsmith_watch` already did. The watch's `PhoneLink` reads the connected nodes and which of them have it, every time the screen opens, whenever the capability changes, and every 15 s while no phone with the app is found.
- **What the idle screen says** (`WorkoutActivity`): phone with Liftsmith, "No workout running" with **Start on phone**, which opens `liftsmith://start` on the phone (the voice shortcut's link, so it opens the app and starts today's workout, which then shows on the watch); phone without Liftsmith, "Get Liftsmith on your phone" with **Install on phone**, which opens the app's Play page on the phone; no phone, "Phone not connected · Keep your phone nearby with Bluetooth on." After a tap it says "Check your phone", or what to do by hand when the phone couldn't be reached. Both open through `RemoteActivityHelper`, the watch-to-phone way Google's guidelines name.
- **During a workout with the phone out of reach** the screen says "Phone not connected" under the buttons, so Log set doesn't fail quietly.
- **Messages go to phones with Liftsmith** (the capability's nodes), falling back to every connected node until that's known.
- **The phone sends the watch's state before starting its notification service**, so a service that can't start never keeps the watch behind.
- **Play Console**: the Wear OS release type is turned off until the fixed bundle is ready, so the phone release isn't held by it (Melvin, 2026-11-20). Turn it back on (Test and release › Advanced settings › Release types › Wear OS, Opt in), then the next Release run uploads the Wear bundle again and it can go for review.
- **Apple Watch**: nothing to change. It ships inside the iPhone app, so it can't be installed without its phone app, and watchOS can't open the iPhone app from the watch ([Platform deltas](platform-deltas.md)).

## Garmin (Connect IQ app, 2026-11-10)

Melvin asked for every "coming later" connection to be built (2026-11-10), and Garmin was the first.

- **Same job as the other two watch apps**: the set, weight and reps or the rest countdown, plates or what's next, heart rate, Log set and +30 s, a buzz when rest ends, "No workout running" otherwise. No planning or history on the watch. Code in `garmin/` (Monkey C), with build and store steps in `garmin/README.md`.
- **Buttons, not on-screen buttons**: Garmin watches are driven by their keys, so START (or a tap on touch watches) is Log set and DOWN (or a swipe up) is +30 s during rest, with a hint line at the bottom saying which. BACK leaves the app.
- **Phone side**: Garmin's Connect IQ companion SDK on both phones (`com.garmin.connectiq:ciq-companion-app-sdk` on Android, the `connectiq-companion-app-sdk-ios` Swift package on iOS), in `GarminLink.java` / `GarminLink.swift`, called from the same `WorkoutSession.update` and `stop` as the Wear OS and Apple Watch links. Messages use short keys (`t`, `s`, `n`, `p`, `r`, `h`, `v`; `GarminMessages` on both platforms, unit tested on Android) because Connect IQ messages are small and slow. **Rest goes as seconds left**, not a time, so the watch's clock never has to agree with the phone's. Heart-rate-only changes go at most every 10 s; anything else goes straight away.
- **Taps and heart rate** come back as the same broadcasts (Android) and callbacks (iOS) as the Wear OS and Apple Watch apps, so the web app treats them identically: `log` and `add30` only, heart rate 30 to 240 and sent at most every 5 s.
- **Opening**: the first update of a workout asks each connected Garmin watch to open the app (the watch asks the lifter, which is Garmin's rule).
- **Finding the watch**: Android gets the paired watches from Garmin Connect by itself. iOS can't, so Connected apps › Garmin watch has **Choose my watch**, which opens Garmin Connect's picker; it comes back on `liftsmith-ciq://` and the picked watches are kept on the phone. The iOS SDK talks Bluetooth itself, so it only starts once a watch has been picked (lifters without a Garmin never see a Bluetooth prompt from it). Both need the Garmin Connect app.
- **Connected apps** shows each watch: ready, needs the Liftsmith watch app, or not connected. Until the store listing exists it says the app is on its way; `GARMIN_STORE_URL` in `src/lib/garmin.ts` turns on a "Get the watch app" button. The web app says the Garmin app works with the phone apps.
- **Watches**: recent ones with Connect IQ 3.2 or later and a heart rate sensor (Forerunner 165 to 970, fēnix 7 and 8, epix 2, Enduro 3, Instinct 3 AMOLED, Venu 2 to 4 and X1, vívoactive 5 and 6). Add more in `garmin/manifest.xml`.
- **Words**: six languages in `garmin/resources*/`, checked by `nativeStrings.test.ts`.
- **Builds**: Garmin's compiler needs device files that only download after a Garmin sign-in, so CI doesn't build the watch app; Melvin builds and uploads `garmin.iq` (steps in `garmin/README.md`). CI builds both phone apps with the SDK.

## Rep counting from the wrist (Labs, 2026-11-11)

Melvin picked it from the Labs ideas (2026-11-11: "Labs ideas 1 to 5").

- **What it does**: with **Settings › Labs › Watch rep counting** on (Pro, like every lab), the Wear OS or Apple Watch app counts reps from wrist motion during each set, and the count fills in the reps for Log set on the phone, exactly as camera counting does. The watch already shows the set's reps (`next`), so it sees the count go up too. A small "Watch: 5 reps" pill sits on the exercise card once the watch has sent anything for the set; nothing changes during rest. No extra tap: logging stays one tap.
- **The phone counts, the watch measures.** While a set is under way (lab on, not resting, the camera not counting, a lift the wrist moves in: `imuProfile`), the phone puts `motion: true` in the workout state it already sends. The watch then streams its fused vertical acceleration (m/s², up positive, gravity removed) and rotation rate (rad/s) at 50 a second, in batches about every 250 ms: `{"t": first reading's ms, "s": [ms after t, up, rot, …]}`, at most 200 readings. On Wear OS that's `MotionStream` and `MotionBatch` (linear acceleration projected on the gravity sensor, plus the gyroscope) over the message path `/liftsmith/motion`; on Apple Watch it's `MotionStream` in `WatchModel.swift` (Core Motion device motion) as a WatchConnectivity message, only while the phone is reachable. The phones check each batch (`WatchMessages.motion` on both) and hand it to the web app as a `watchMotion` event, where `src/lib/reps/watchMotion.ts` checks it again and feeds the same motion counter phone motion uses (`ImuRepCounter.pushFused`). One counter means both watches count alike and it's tested once, against the simulated sets in `reps.test.ts`.
- **Why the wrist, not the phone**: the motion research (rep-counter notes, 2026-11-09) found fused motion counted 88 to 100% of simulated reps, and a wrist moves with the bar on most lifts, where a phone in a pocket doesn't. Lifts where the hands stay still (pull-ups, dips, planks, carries, push-ups) aren't counted.
- **Battery**: the sensors run only during a set with the lab on; between sets and without the lab nothing is read, and nothing is sent when no watch has the app.
- **Gaps**: Wear OS streams from its workout service, which needs the heart rate permission. Garmin isn't included (Connect IQ messages are too small and slow). The web app has no watch, so the lab only shows in the phone apps. The Apple Watch half hasn't been tried on a watch yet (open items, iOS deferred).

## Hands-free watch sets (Labs, 2026-11-26)

Melvin, 2026-11-26: "Can liftsmith automatically track workouts on the wear os app? Not using the button presses??" He picked "Fully hands-free sets" over Health Services heart rate and over recognizing the lift from the wrist (research-grade, needs a trained model).

- **What it does**: **Settings › Labs › Hands-free watch sets** (`labWatchAuto`, Pro like every lab, `native`, needs watch rep counting) runs the whole set from the wrist. After the first 15 seconds of rest (`SETTLE_MS`: racking, walking away, shaking out), the watch keeps streaming its motion; once the phone has counted 3 reps (`START_REPS`, so a sip of water isn't a set), rest ends at once (`skipRest`) and those reps carry into the set (`withCarry`). The set then logs itself a few seconds after the last rep with the hands-free sets rule (`handsFree.ts`), whether or not that lab is on, and rest starts. The watch taps once when the set starts by itself and once when it logs itself. Undo puts a set back as usual. Rules in `src/lib/watchAuto.ts`, wired in `Workout.tsx`.
- **The phone still counts.** Same split as watch rep counting: the phone asks with `motion` plus `motionRest` (keep streaming through rest), and the watch says nothing back but motion. The buzz is a `cue` id in the workout state that changes each time the set starts or logs itself; the watch taps when it sees a new one in the same workout (`WorkoutState.newCue` on Wear OS, `WatchState.newCue` on Apple Watch), only when vibrate is on. A watch app from before this change ignores both fields and stops streaming during rest, so nothing breaks: rest just ends on its timer.
- **Not for**: lifts the wrist doesn't move in (pull-ups, dips, planks, carries), cardio, warm-ups and superset rounds during rest (those still end rest on the timer), and while the camera or phone motion is counting.
- **Platforms**: Wear OS and Apple Watch get it together. Garmin doesn't stream motion, so it has neither watch rep counting nor this (Platform deltas). The phone app has to be running for the count, as with watch rep counting.
- **Play review**: built on 2026-11-26 while the Wear OS app was in production review. The change only reaches Play's internal track through the usual release; nothing was sent to production.
- **Untested on a wrist.** The 15 seconds and 3 reps are guesses, like the hands-free waits; tune them on real sets before it leaves Labs.

## Today on the watch face (2026-11-26)

Melvin picked it from the watch ideas (2026-11-26, idea 3: "a tile/complication showing today's call ... with Start on phone").

- **What it shows**: today's call (the readiness call's label, or the program day's name, or Rest day), the minutes and "Readiness N" when the readiness signals moved anything, plus the short reason where there's room. A workout in progress takes over as before.
- **One source**: the home-screen widget's snapshot (`widgetSnapshot` in `src/lib/widget.ts`, which now carries a `watch` part: `call`, `mins`, `ready`). The app sends it only when a line changes, and `WorkoutSession.setWidget` on each phone hands it to the watches too (`WatchMessages.today` on both phones keeps day, kind, title, why, stale, call, mins and ready; an empty snapshot clears it). Nothing new runs on a timer.
- **Wear OS**: a Data Layer item of its own, `/liftsmith/today`, apart from the live workout, kept on the watch (`TodayStore`). A **tile** (`TodayTileService`, Tiles 1.5 with Material layouts) shows the label, the call, the minutes and readiness, the reason and **Start on phone**, which opens the watch app with `startOnPhone` and that opens the phone's `liftsmith://start` (the same path as the idle screen's button). A **complication** (`TodayComplicationService`, short and long text) shows the call; a tap opens the watch app, never a start, because a watch face tap is easy to make by accident. The watch app's idle screen shows the same call.
- **Apple Watch**: the application context now holds the live workout (`json`) and today's call (`today`) together, merged, because each update replaces the whole context. A complication on the face gets `transferCurrentComplicationUserInfo` (budgeted by watchOS). A WidgetKit extension, `LiftsmithWatchWidgets`, draws circular, corner, inline and rectangular complications from what the watch app saved (`WatchToday.swift`, in both targets), with a timeline entry at midnight. **No Start on phone**: watchOS can't open the iPhone app, so a tap opens the watch app with today's call. The watch app and the extension share it through the App Group `group.com.getliftsmith.app`, which isn't on either target yet (open items, iOS deferred); until it is, the complication shows "Liftsmith" and the watch app's own idle screen still shows the call.
- **Garmin**: a **glance** (`TodayGlance.mc`) and the idle screen show the call. It comes in a message under `g` (`GarminMessages.today` and `WatchMessages.garminToday`) so an older watch app never takes it for a workout; a background service (`TodayService.mc`, Background permission) stores it when the app isn't open, so the app has to be opened once on the watch. Not built in CI (see below), so untried.
- **Never yesterday's**: every copy carries its day (`y-m-d` like `dayKey`), and a watch that reaches midnight shows nothing until the phone sends the new day's plan.
