# Watch apps (Wear OS and Apple Watch)

Part of the decision record. [AGENTS.md](../../AGENTS.md) has the rules that apply to every change and the list of topics. Record decisions about this topic here, in the same change as the code.

Melvin approved building them on 2026-11-10 ("Yes to all", after the list of what was still missing).

## What the watch does

- **The phone's workout, on the wrist.** The watch shows exactly what the live notification and the Live Activity show, from the same `WorkoutSession.update` call (`src/lib/sessionNotify.ts`): the set ("Set 2 of 4"), the exercise, the weight and reps, the plates, or during rest a big countdown with the next set under it, plus heart rate. Two buttons: **Log set** (always) and **+30 s** (during rest). Nothing else: no planning, no settings, no history. The phone stays the one place where a workout is planned and logged, so there's no second copy of the engine and nothing to merge.
- **Taps are the notification's buttons.** Log set and +30 s from the watch reach the web app as the same `action` events the notification and Live Activity buttons send (`onSessionAction` in `App.tsx`), so they log the set as planned and start rest the same way. The phone then sends the new state back. The phone only accepts `log` and `add30` from a watch (`WatchMessages` on both platforms).
- **Heart rate from the watch's own sensor.** While a workout runs, the watch reads heart rate and sends it to the phone at most every 2 s, where it arrives as a `heartRate` event (`onWatchHeartRate`). A Bluetooth monitor (a strap, or a watch broadcasting its heart rate) still wins when one is live; otherwise a watch reading counts while it's under 10 s old (`pickBpm` in `src/lib/watchHr.ts`). Watch readings go into the workout's heart rate log only while no monitor is live, so one workout never mixes two streams. The finished workout's average and maximum, and the write to Health Connect or Apple Health, then work exactly as with a strap.
- **A buzz when rest ends**, on the wrist, if the lifter's rest-over alert vibrates (Workout preferences). It follows the same `vibrate` flag the phone uses.
- **Opens itself when a workout starts.** The first update of a new workout (`sessionId`) opens the watch app once: on Apple Watch through `HKHealthStore.startWatchApp` (the system's own way for a phone app to start a watch workout), on Wear OS through `RemoteActivityHelper` to watches that have our app (the `liftsmith_watch` capability). After that the lifter can leave it; the watch keeps up in the background.
- **When the workout ends** (End, Discard or the automatic end of a forgotten workout, which all call `stopSession`), the phone clears the watch's state and the watch goes back to "No workout running · Start one on your phone and it shows here." A state the phone stopped updating more than 3 hours ago is treated as ended, the same limit as the phone's auto-end.
- **No workout is saved from the watch.** The phone already writes the finished workout to Health Connect or Apple Health, so the Apple Watch's workout session is discarded at the end rather than saved twice.
- **Starting a workout from the watch** isn't offered: the phone app has to be open for its plan, and neither platform lets a watch open a phone app in the foreground. Voice and system shortcuts on the phone cover a hands-free start.

## How it's built

- **The message**: one JSON object with the notification's fields (`title`, `setLabel`, `next`, `plates`, `restUntil`, `hr`, `vibrate`, `done`, `total`, `sessionId`) plus `at` (when it was sent). Same keys on both platforms.
- **Wear OS** (`android/wear`, a second Gradle module, Java and plain views like the rest of the native code; Wear OS 3+, API 30). Same application id (`com.getliftsmith.app`) and signing key as the phone app, which the Data Layer needs. The phone's `WatchLink.java` puts the state in one Data Layer item, `/liftsmith/workout`, which the system keeps in step and delivers to a watch that was asleep or out of range as soon as it's back; ending deletes it. The watch's `PhoneListenerService` hands changes to `WatchHub`, the watch's single source of truth, which the screen (`WorkoutActivity`) and `WorkoutService` listen to. Taps and heart rate go back as messages (`/liftsmith/action`, `/liftsmith/hr`) to the phone's `WatchListenerService`, which uses the notification buttons' broadcast. `WorkoutService` is a foreground service of type `health`: it keeps the app alive with the screen off, reads `Sensor.TYPE_HEART_RATE`, and shows the workout as an **Ongoing Activity** (its icon on the watch face). It needs the heart rate permission (`android.permission.health.READ_HEART_RATE` on Wear OS 6, `BODY_SENSORS` before), asked once the first time a workout shows; without it the watch still shows the workout and logs sets.
- **Apple Watch** (`ios/App/LiftsmithWatch`, a single-target SwiftUI watch app embedded in the iPhone app, watchOS 10+, bundle id `com.getliftsmith.app.watchkitapp`). The phone's `WatchLink.swift` sends the state as the WatchConnectivity application context (always the latest, delivered when the watch wakes) and as a message while the watch is reachable. Taps go back as messages, or as queued user info when the phone isn't reachable, so a tap is never lost. `WatchModel` runs an `HKWorkoutSession` (traditional strength training, indoor) while a workout shows: it gives live heart rate and keeps the app running with the wrist down. The watch app has the HealthKit capability and the `workout-processing` background mode.
- **Words** follow the watch's language: English, Spanish, French, German, Italian and Japanese in `android/wear/src/main/res/values*/strings.xml` and `ios/App/LiftsmithWatch/*.lproj/Localizable.strings`. Everything else on screen comes from the phone, already in the app's language.
- **Look**: always dark (the app's dark tokens), the brand blue for the set label, the primary gradient on Log set (Wear OS), the system's prominent button in the brand tint (watchOS), red for heart rate, tabular numbers. The icon is the app's own, copied by `scripts/make-icons.mjs`.
- **Builds**: CI's Android job builds both apps and runs both modules' unit tests (`WorkoutStateTest`, `WatchMessagesTest`) and uploads the Wear APK; the iOS job builds the iPhone app with the watch app embedded. `release.yml` builds the Wear OS bundle with the phone's (version code + 100,000,000, because every bundle of one Play app needs its own) and uploads it to Play's **Wear OS internal** track (`wear:internal`); the Apple Watch app goes to TestFlight inside the iPhone app.

## Setup steps in the stores

- **Play Console:** Wear OS has to be added as a form factor once (Test and release › Advanced settings › Form factors › Add Wear OS) before Play takes a Wear bundle. Until then the release's Wear upload step fails on its own (`continue-on-error`) and the phone release goes ahead. Wear OS apps are reviewed against Google's Wear OS quality guidelines, which need watch screenshots in the listing.
- **App Store Connect:** nothing extra. The archive's automatic signing (`-allowProvisioningUpdates`) registers the watch app's bundle id with HealthKit the first time. If it can't, register `com.getliftsmith.app.watchkitapp` with the HealthKit capability in the Apple Developer portal.

## Garmin (Connect IQ app, 2026-11-10)

Melvin asked for every "coming later" connection to be built (2026-11-10), and Garmin was the first.

- **Same job as the other two watch apps**: the set, weight and reps or the rest countdown, plates or what's next, heart rate, Log set and +30 s, a buzz when rest ends, "No workout running" otherwise. No planning or history on the watch. Code in `garmin/` (Monkey C), with build and store steps in `garmin/README.md`.
- **Buttons, not on-screen buttons**: Garmin watches are driven by their keys, so START (or a tap on touch watches) is Log set and DOWN (or a swipe up) is +30 s during rest, with a hint line at the bottom saying which. BACK leaves the app.
- **Phone side**: Garmin's Connect IQ companion SDK on both phones (`com.garmin.connectiq:ciq-companion-app-sdk` on Android, the `connectiq-companion-app-sdk-ios` Swift package on iOS), in `GarminLink.java` / `GarminLink.swift`, called from the same `WorkoutSession.update` and `stop` as the Wear OS and Apple Watch links. Messages use short keys (`t`, `s`, `n`, `p`, `r`, `h`, `v`; `GarminMessages` on both platforms, unit tested on Android) because Connect IQ messages are small and slow. **Rest goes as seconds left**, not a time, so the watch's clock never has to agree with the phone's. Heart-rate-only changes go at most every 10 s; anything else goes straight away.
- **Taps and heart rate** come back as the same broadcasts (Android) and callbacks (iOS) as the Wear OS and Apple Watch apps, so the web app treats them identically: `log` and `add30` only, heart rate 30 to 240 and sent at most every 5 s.
- **Opening**: the first update of a workout asks each connected Garmin watch to open the app (the watch asks the lifter, which is Garmin's rule).
- **Finding the watch**: Android gets the paired watches from Garmin Connect by itself. iOS can't, so Connected apps › Garmin watch has **Choose my watch**, which opens Garmin Connect's picker; it comes back on `liftsmith-ciq://` and the picked watches are kept on the phone. The iOS SDK talks Bluetooth itself, so it only starts once a watch has been picked (lifters without a Garmin never see a Bluetooth prompt from it). Both need the Garmin Connect app.
- **Connected apps** shows each watch: ready, needs the Liftsmith watch app, or not connected. Until the store listing exists it says the app is on its way; `GARMIN_STORE_URL` in `src/lib/garmin.ts` turns on a "Get the watch app" button. The web app says the Garmin app works with the phone apps.
- **Watches**: recent ones with Connect IQ 3.2 or later and a heart rate sensor (Forerunner 165 to 970, fēnix 7 and 8, epix 2, Enduro 3, Instinct 3 AMOLED, Venu 2 to 4 and X1, vívoactive 5 and 6). Add more in `garmin/manifest.xml`.
- **Words**: six languages in `garmin/resources*/`, checked by `nativeStrings.test.ts`.
- **Builds**: Garmin's compiler needs device files that only download after a Garmin sign-in, so CI doesn't build the watch app; Melvin builds and uploads `garmin.iq` (steps in `garmin/README.md`). CI builds both phone apps with the SDK.
