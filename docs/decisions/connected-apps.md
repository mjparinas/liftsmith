## The page

- **Settings › Connected apps** (`#/connections`, `Connections.tsx`). Settings has one row that opens it (the Settings restructure keeps that one row in its main list). It's the one place to check, sync and fix every integration. Sections, top to bottom:
  1. **Health Connect** (Android) / **Apple Health** (iOS): connected or not, last synced (`ls.health.last`, written by each import), **Sync now** (runs both imports and says how many new readings came in), and what's allowed. Android lists every read and write with a tick (`healthPermissions`, from the plugin's `checkAuthorization`) and **Change permissions** opens Health Connect's own screen (or its install page when it's missing). Apple Health never tells an app which reads were allowed, so iOS says so and explains where to change it (Health app › Apps › Liftsmith); "connected" there means a sync has run or a write was allowed. A line names what reaches us through the health store: smart scales, the Hume Body Pod, Oura, Samsung Health, Garmin, Fitbit and most watches. The web app says Health is in the phone apps.
  2. **Heart rate monitor:** what it does, the last monitor used on this device (`ls.hr.last`, saved by `connectHeartRate`), and **Test my monitor**, which connects and shows live bpm so a strap can be checked before the gym. The workout screen's heart button is still where it's paired for a workout. Browsers without Web Bluetooth get a line saying so.
  3. **Fitness services: Strava, Garmin Connect, Oura, WHOOP, Withings, Polar, Concept2** (Withings, Polar and Concept2 added 2026-11-12, Melvin picked them from a list of new connections; Garmin Connect added 2026-11-15, Melvin: "can you do 2", connecting Garmin, Strava and Whoop). **Pro** since 2026-11-12 (`cloudConnections`, [Money](money.md)): without Pro, Connect opens the paywall and a connected service pauses until Pro returns; Disconnect always works. They need an account (the roadmap's "connecting one explains that it creates a free account"), so a signed-out lifter sees what each does and one **Sign in or create an account** button. Signed in, each row shows connected (Strava shows the athlete's name), not connected, or **Coming soon** when the server has no keys for it yet, with Connect or Disconnect. Services that can be connected (or already are) are listed first and the **Coming soon** ones go to the bottom (Melvin, 2026-11-12), each group in the usual order.
  4. **Garmin watch** (2026-11-10): each Garmin watch the phone knows, and whether it's ready, needs the Liftsmith watch app, or isn't connected; **Choose my watch** on iOS (Garmin Connect's picker). Details in [Watch apps](watch-apps.md).
  5. There is no "Coming later" list any more (2026-11-11): Melvin asked for every item on it to be built, and chose how ("Camera bar speed; Samsung via Health"). The Garmin watch app got its own card; **Samsung Health** keeps coming in through Health Connect, and the Android Health Connect card has a line saying how to switch that on in Samsung Health (`conn.health.samsung`), because Samsung's own Data SDK needs a partner approval and gives nothing Health Connect doesn't already carry; **bar speed** comes from the camera as a lab (Settings › Labs › Camera bar speed, [Camera rep counting](camera-rep-counting.md)) instead of a Bluetooth sensor, because the sensors' protocols are closed and few lifters own one.

## Bluetooth smart scale (Labs, 2026-11-12)

- Melvin picked "Bluetooth smart scales" from the list of new connections. It's a Pro lab (`labScale`), like Bluetooth gym machines, because only scales that share the **standard** Bluetooth services work: the Weight Scale Service (0x181D, Weight Measurement 0x2A9D) and the Body Composition Service (0x181B, Body Composition Measurement 0x2A9C). Many cheap scales use their own app's closed protocol and never show in the picker; scales that sync to Health Connect, Apple Health or Withings already come in that way.
- **Where:** a **Weigh in on my scale** button above the weigh-in (`WeighInForm`, so Progress and the health twin's Weight and Body fat sheets get it together). It opens the picker filtered to either service, connects, says "Step on … now", and waits up to 60 s. The first weight, plus body fat when the scale sends it (within 4 s, often a second packet), fill in the weight box and the body fat box; the lifter checks and taps **Log**, so a wrong reading is never saved. Then the scale is let go.
- **Reading the bytes** (`src/lib/scale.ts`): weight in 0.005 kg or 0.01 lb by the unit flag, `0xFFFF` is a failed measurement; body composition has 16-bit flags, body fat in 0.1 %, then optional fields in the spec's fixed order before an optional weight. Only 25 to 350 kg and 2 to 70 % are kept.
- **Which scales work** (Melvin, 2026-11-12: "make a link in the app to a page on the website that lists all compatible devices"). A **Which scales work?** link under the button opens `/works-with/scales/` (data in `src/content/scales.ts`, linked from the features page's connections table). The lab only subscribes to the two measurements; it doesn't register a user on the scale through the User Data Service (0x181C). The open-source openScale project's handlers (checked 2026-11-12) show that many standard scales, Beurer BF600 and friends, Sanitas SBF72 and friends and the Renpho ES-WBE28, send readings only after that step, so the page lists them as "Standard, but not yet", and only scales with no user step (A&D UC-352BLE, Samsung Smart Scale, Redmond SkyBalance) as "Should work". No model has been tried with Liftsmith yet: move one to `TESTED_SCALES` once someone does. Everything else goes through the scale's own app and Health Connect or Apple Health. Adding User Data Service registration would move the "not yet" group over, with body fat.
- **Platforms:** the same native Bluetooth plugin as heart rate and gym machines on Android and iOS (no new Java or Swift; iOS's Bluetooth permission text now mentions the scale in all six languages); Web Bluetooth in Chrome on the web ([Platform deltas](platform-deltas.md)).

## Workouts saved to Health Connect and Apple Health (2026-11-10)

- The health plugin can only write samples, so finished workouts used to arrive as loose calories and heart rate. Our own small plugin now writes each one as a real workout: **`HealthWorkoutPlugin.kt`** (Android, the app module's first Kotlin file, because Health Connect's client is Kotlin-first; it asks only for `WRITE_EXERCISE` and writes a strength training `ExerciseSessionRecord` whose client record id is the workout id, so a second write updates it) and **`HealthWorkoutPlugin.swift`** (iOS, `HKWorkoutBuilder` with a traditional strength training workout, `HKMetadataKeyExternalUUID` set to the workout id and skipped if it's already there). Both are called `HealthWorkout` (`status`, `request`, `save`) from `saveWorkout` in `health.ts`.
- Title and notes are the same as Strava's (`stravaWorkout`): "Strength training" and the sets, one line per exercise. iOS has no notes field, so only the title goes in (as the brand name).
- **Calories and heart rate:** on iOS they go inside the workout (so the Move ring counts them once, and the plugin's separate samples are skipped); on Android they stay separate records over the same time, which is how Health Connect apps link them. Without the workout permission, both platforms write the samples as before.
- The permission is asked for right after the health plugin's sheet when Health is connected (`connectHealth`), and Connected apps shows "Workouts (written)" with a **Save my workouts to …** button for lifters who connected before this existed.
- Play: `WRITE_EXERCISE` must be added to the Health Connect declaration in Play Console (docs/PRIVACY_LABELS.md). The privacy policy and `public/privacypolicy.html` say workouts are saved with their time, title and sets.

## Strava, Garmin Connect, Oura, WHOOP, Withings, Polar and Concept2: the server

- **Routes** (server build only): `GET /api/connect/` (which services have keys, which this account connected), `POST /api/connect/<service>/` (a one-time sign-in link), `DELETE /api/connect/<service>/` (disconnect), `GET /api/connect/<service>/callback/` (the service sends the lifter back), `GET /api/connect/<oura|whoop|withings|polar>/daily/?days=45&tz=…` (readings), `GET /api/connect/concept2/results/?days=90&tz=…` (sessions), `POST /api/connect/strava/upload/` (one workout). All but the callback need the account's Bearer token. Code: `src/server/connect.ts`; shared types `src/lib/cloud.ts`; app side `src/lib/connections.ts`.
- **Sign-in flow:** the app asks for a link while signed in; the server stores a random one-time state (15 minutes, `connect_states`) with the account and platform and returns the service's authorize URL. The app opens it (`openOutside`: on Android and iOS a navigation to another site goes to the browser). The callback uses up the state, swaps the code for tokens and stores them. The website goes straight back to `/app/#/connections/<service>`; the phone apps get a small page with **Back to Liftsmith** (`liftsmith://connected`, a link both apps already open, ignored by the shortcut handlers), and the page refreshes its status when the app comes to the front. No native code was needed.
- **Tokens** live in `connections`, one row per account and service, **encrypted** with AES-256-GCM under a key made from that service's client secret, so a copy of the database alone can't use them (changing a secret means lifters connect again). They refresh 2 minutes before they run out (Whoop asks for `offline` again on refresh). **Disconnect** revokes at the service (Strava deauthorize, Oura revoke, WHOOP's user access delete) and deletes the row; **deleting the account** does the same for every service (`forgetAll` in `deleteAccount`).
- **Scopes:** Strava `activity:write,read`; Oura `daily heartrate`; WHOOP `offline read:recovery read:sleep`; Withings `user.metrics,user.activity`; Polar `accesslink.read_all`; Concept2 `user:read,results:read`. Nothing more, so the consent pages ask for little.
- **How the three newer services differ** (2026-11-12): **Withings** wraps every answer in `{ status, body }` (HTTP 200 even on failure, status 0 when it worked) and every token call carries `action=requesttoken`. **Polar** takes the client id and secret as HTTP Basic auth on the token call, gives a long-lived token with no refresh, and shares nothing until the app registers the lifter (`POST /v3/users` with a hash of the account id as the member id; 409 means already registered). Its user id is kept in `connections.remote_user` so Disconnect can remove the lifter (`DELETE /v3/users/<id>`), which is how AccessLink ends access. **Withings and Concept2 have no plain revoke**, so Disconnect forgets the tokens and the privacy policy says the lifter can also remove Liftsmith in their account there.
- **Strava gets finished workouts.** When a workout ends (`Workout.tsx` → `queueStrava`), the app builds the activity from the events (`stravaWorkout`: "Strength training", `WeightTraining`, start as local clock time, elapsed time, and the sets, one line per exercise, from the language catalogue) and puts it in an outbox in localStorage, so a workout finished offline posts later (on open, return to the front, or back online). The server records each posted session (`connect_uploads`), so a retry never posts twice. A switch on the Strava row turns posting off (`profile.connections.stravaPost`, missing means on). A 409 (Strava removed the access) empties the outbox and refreshes the status.
- **Oura and WHOOP send readings in.** The app asks at most every 3 hours (and on Sync now) for the last 45 days; the server fetches them and maps them to the health twin's metrics without storing them: Oura's sleep periods (the night's total sleep; the main sleep's average HRV, lowest heart rate as resting heart rate, and breathing rate) and finished days' steps; WHOOP's scored sleeps (in bed minus awake, the night's breathing rate) and recoveries (HRV RMSSD, resting heart rate), each on the local day the night ended (WHOOP's own time zone offset, or the phone's). The app stores them as `health` events with ids `oura-…`/`whoop-…` (`importCloudReadings`), so the health twin and the readiness call use them like Health's, re-imports add nothing, and they sync with the lifter's account like every other health reading.
- **Withings and Polar send readings in** the same way: Withings' weigh-ins (measure type 1) and body fat (type 6) become one weigh-in a day, `withings-bw-<day>` (the day's last reading wins; goals the lifter typed, category 2, are skipped), and its finished days' steps and nights' sleep summary (total sleep, lowest heart rate as resting heart rate, average breathing) become health readings. Polar's nightly recharge (average HRV and breathing; its average overnight heart rate stands in for resting heart rate, because Polar gives no lowest) and sleep (light + deep + REM) become health readings; AccessLink only keeps 28 days. Weigh-ins update the profile's body weight when they're the newest, and refresh TDEE (`cloudReadingEvents` in `src/lib/cloudImport.ts`).
- **Concept2 sends sessions in.** Rowing (and its dynamic and slides models), SkiErg and BikeErg results from the Logbook become finished cardio workouts, like a hand-logged past workout: a manual session start under the active gym, one set on `rowing-machine`, `ski-erg` or `stationary-bike` (reps are the seconds, with `distanceM`) and an end, ids `c2-<logbook id>`, so nothing is added twice. The Logbook logs local time with an IANA zone, which the server turns into a moment (`zonedTime`). The first pull asks for the last year, later ones the last 30 days, at most every 3 hours or on Sync now (`cloudCardioEvents`).
- **Without keys a service shows "Coming soon"** and its routes answer 503, so the page can ship before each developer app is approved. Each service also caps a new app at a few users until it's reviewed (Strava, Oura and WHOOP each list 10 or so); apply early (roadmap item 4).

## Garmin Connect (2026-11-15)

- **Why now:** Garmin is the service serious lifters ask for most. Before this, Garmin data only reached Liftsmith through Health Connect or Apple Health (when the lifter turned on sharing in Garmin Connect), and the Garmin watch app ([Watch apps](watch-apps.md)) only shows the workout on the wrist. Garmin Connect is a fitness service row like the others: Pro, needs an account, "Coming soon" until `GARMIN_CLIENT_ID` and `GARMIN_CLIENT_SECRET` are set. Its app-side words are `conn.garminConnect.*`, because `conn.garmin.*` already belongs to the watch card.
- **Sign-in:** the Garmin Connect Developer Program's OAuth 2.0 with **PKCE** (S256): the code verifier is made with the one-time state and kept in `connect_states.verifier`, and the token call sends it. There is no scope: the lifter picks what to share on Garmin's own page. After the token swap the server asks Garmin for the lifter's Garmin user id (`/wellness-api/rest/user/id`, kept in `connections.remote_user`, needed to know whose a ping is; no id, no connection) and asks for the last 30 days again (`/backfill/<type>`, best effort; a lifter who didn't allow historical data gets only new days).
- **Garmin pushes rather than answers.** Unlike the other services there's no "give me the last 45 days": Garmin notifies our endpoint when a watch syncs. Liftsmith uses **ping mode**: `POST /api/connect/garmin/push/` gets each type's entries with a user id and a callback URL, checks the URL is `https://apis.garmin.com/wellness-api/rest/…`, and fetches it with that lifter's own token. Data sent inline (push mode) is ignored, because a notification can't be signed and anyone could post one; fetching it back from Garmin means only Garmin's data is ever kept. A `deregistrations` entry (the lifter removed Liftsmith in Garmin Connect) deletes the connection and everything kept. The endpoint always answers 200 quickly, because Garmin retries anything else.
- **What comes in:** dailies (steps, resting heart rate), sleeps (deep + light + REM, or the whole sleep less time awake), HRV (last night's average), body composition (weight and body fat from an Index scale or typed into Garmin Connect, a weigh-in on the day measured in the lifter's own offset) and activities: runs (`outdoor-run`), treadmill runs (`treadmill-run`), rides (`outdoor-cycling`), indoor and virtual rides (`stationary-bike`), indoor rows (`rowing-machine`), elliptical and stair sessions (`elliptical`, `stair-climber`, time only). Strength sessions are left out: those are Liftsmith's own. Walks too, for now, so a day's errands don't become workouts.
- **Kept until the app pulls them:** readings in `cloud_readings` (one row per metric and day, a later ping replaces it) and sessions in `cloud_sessions`. Readings are deleted after 100 days and sessions after a year; Disconnect, a deregistration and deleting the account delete them at once. The app reads them through the same routes as the others (`/daily/` and `/results/`), so `pullCloud` treats Garmin like Oura for readings and like Concept2 for sessions (event ids `garmin-…`). Today's steps wait until the day is over, as with Oura. This is the one service whose data is stored on our server for a while; the privacy policy says so.
- **No doubles:** a Garmin session that overlaps a workout already on the device is skipped (`cloudCardioEvents`' `busy`), because the watch also records the cardio part of a Liftsmith workout and a row the Concept2 Logbook already sent.
- **Disconnect** deletes the lifter's registration at Garmin (`DELETE /wellness-api/rest/user/registration`), which ends our access.
- **Not doing, unless Melvin reopens it:** Garmin's Training API (sending a planned workout to the watch's own workout list) and posting finished lifting sessions to Garmin Connect. The Connect IQ watch app already shows the live workout, and Garmin gives no public way to upload a finished activity, so neither was worth the extra permission. This is a decision, not a leftover task ([Open items](open-items.md)). Weekly email and blog post: once Garmin leaves "Coming soon" (a lifter can't act on it before), add a `garmin-connect` weekly email and a post, like the Strava and WHOOP ones will need.
