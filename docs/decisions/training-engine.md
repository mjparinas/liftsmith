# Training engine (src/lib)

Part of the decision record. [AGENTS.md](../../AGENTS.md) has the rules that apply to every change and the list of topics. Record decisions about this topic here, in the same change as the code.

- **Equipment and gym profiles.** Each gym has its own equipment list, bars, the exact plates owned (with counts), dumbbells, kettlebells and machine step. The generator uses only exercises whose every needed item is at the active gym (`available` in `exercises.ts`). **Dumbbells and kettlebells are editable** (Melvin, 2026-11-11: "How do I customize dumb bells?"; they used to be fixed by the gym type). On Gyms, each has a card with its range that opens one sheet (`GymWeights.tsx`, helpers in `weightSets.ts`): start from a preset (dumbbells: gym rack, home set, adjustable 2.5 to 24 kg or 5 to 52.5 lb; kettlebells: standard or full set), fill a range from lightest, heaviest and step, tap a weight to drop or restore it (dropped weights stay on screen while the sheet is open, so a wrong tap is one tap to undo), or add one odd weight. Workouts round to the weights kept (`roundLoad` and `stepLoad` in `loads.ts`). Pure web code, so Android, iOS and the web app get it together.
- **The library is growing toward 1,000 exercises, in batches** (Melvin, 2026-11-11, from the feature brainstorm). **Batch 1 took it from 300 to 500**: 201 more across every muscle group (chest and press variations, raises, triceps work, rows, pulldowns, shrugs, pull-up variations, curls and grip work, squats, split squats and lunges, leg press stances, hinges, leg curls, hip thrusts and glute work, adductors, calves, planks and floor core, hanging core, anti-rotation, and eight conditioning moves logged as time). **Batch 2 took it to 754**: 254 more (Smith, cable, landmine, kettlebell and band versions of the presses, raises, rows, curls and pushdowns; squat, lunge, hinge, glute and calf variations; planks, holds, anti-rotation and seated twists; dumbbell, kettlebell and hex bar carries; Olympic lifts and dumbbell power moves; bodyweight skills on rings and the bar; and 33 conditioning moves logged as time, from bike, rower and ski machine sprints to burpee variations, skips and hiking). Rules for each batch: names, setup lines and cues are our own, written in all six languages in the same change; a move named after a person or a brand gets a plain descriptive name (the "rotation press", the "behind-the-body cable curl"); every exercise gets a 3D motion, reusing a family's builder with parameters (`squat`, `rdl`, `benchPress`, `fly`, `curl`, …) until it earns its own; one-dumbbell moves go in `ONE_DUMBBELL`, one-side-at-a-time moves in `PER_SIDE` (`sides.ts`), holds in `TIMED` (`progression.ts`); loads stay honest (a weight plate held in the hands is not a barbell, so those moves use a dumbbell instead), and kettlebell moves use one bell, because a kettlebell set logs one implement. Camera rep counting follows the id (`reps/profiles.ts`): leg curls count from the knee, wrist work and shrugs aren't counted (too small to see), and ids ending in "hang" are holds; so an id doesn't contain "dip" or "curl" unless the move is one. Every new exercise gets its 3D still and share card in the same change (`STILLS=1 npm run build:static && PW_CHROMIUM=/opt/pw-browsers/chromium node scripts/og-images.mjs --missing`; `ogImages.test.ts` checks). The scratch tool that wrote the batches isn't kept; each batch lands as ordinary rows. - **Cardio** (Melvin, 2026-11-11). Pattern `cardio`, and `Exercise.cardio` is `"time"` or `"distance"` (`CARDIO` in `exercises.ts`). The generator never picks it (strength plans stay strength plans); the lifter adds it to a routine or a workout. Reps are seconds (`TIMED`). The first session is 20 minutes, then the target repeats what was done last time, because the lifter sets the pace (`note.cardioFirst`, `note.cardioAgain`). Distance work also logs `distanceM` on the set, pre-filled from the last time or a start per exercise (`CARDIO_START_M`: 3 km runs and walks, 2 km rows and ski, 10 km rides, 100 m sleds). See [Workout screen](workout-screen.md).
- **Bodyweight exercises** cover every major muscle, so a "Bodyweight" gym (hotel, park) gets full workouts. A test checks this.
- **Workout length.** A slider from 15 to 90 minutes. The plan is seeded by day and gym, so moving the slider adds or drops exercises at the end instead of reshuffling (`generator.ts`). **Every length counts warm-up sets and rest** (Melvin, 2026-11-09): an exercise is setup (60 s), its warm-ups with 60 s rest after each, its working sets, and the rest between them, and the rest before each exercise counts too (`itemSeconds`, `planSeconds`). Today shows each exercise's minutes and warm-up count. If the final order's warm-ups push the plan past the time asked for, sets come off the end.
- **Warm-up sets** (Melvin, 2026-11-09; `warmup.ts`). The first loaded lift for a muscle ramps up on weights the gym can make: the empty bar ×10 for barbells, then about 55%×5 and 80%×2 (or 50/70/85% for 100 kg and up). Machines and dumbbells get 50%×8 and 75%×4. A later barbell lift for muscles that are already warm gets one feeler set (70%×3). A first isolation exercise gets one light set, and bodyweight and timed exercises get none. Warm-ups are their own `warmup` event, so stats, recovery, progression, PRs and XP never count them. The workout screen shows "Warm-up 1 of N", with a 60 s rest and a Skip warm-up link.
- **Recovery.** Each set adds fatigue to its primary muscles (full) and secondary muscles (half), less when far from failure. Fatigue fades so 90% is gone after about 48 h for small muscles and 72 h for large ones. Bands: Fresh 90%+, Ready 70%+, Recovering 40%+, Fatigued under 40% (`recovery.ts`). The generator only targets muscles at 55%+ and avoids exercises whose primary muscles are under 40%.
- **Periodization.** Blocks of 3 to 6 build weeks (default 4) then a deload week. RIR steps down across the build weeks (3, 2, 1, 0 for four weeks); the deload is RIR 4 at half the sets. Weekly sets per muscle rise from 8–12 to 12–20 by experience (`block.ts`).
- **Progression.** Double progression on top of the RIR target: hit every set's target and the next session adds a rep; at the top of the rep range, the weight goes up one loadable step and reps go back to the bottom; a miss by 2+ reps drops a step. Post-exercise feedback (too easy, too hard) shifts it one more step (`progression.ts`).
- **Starting lighter after a break** (competitor audit, Melvin 2026-11-09; `src/lib/layoff.ts`). When the muscles an exercise trains (any exercise sharing a primary muscle) haven't been worked for 10+ days, the first session back is about 10% lighter for each whole week away, at most 30%, from the heaviest working weight of that exercise's last session before the break. On adaptive plans the next two sessions close the gap a third at a time (70 → 80 → 90 after three weeks off), unless normal progression is already past it or the last session was a miss or "too hard"; after three sessions back the break is over. The note on the target says why ("Back after 3 weeks off: starting 30% lighter", `note.comeback`, `note.rampBack`). **Fixed programs** get the same first-session cut (the planned sets carry `comeback`, and so do the logged ones), then rebuild with their own jumps from the lighter weight, as the well-known 5×5 apps do. Deload weeks, bodyweight exercises and sets done lighter for an injury are left alone. Derived from events, never stored.
- **One dumbbell or a pair, one side at a time** (competitor audit, Melvin 2026-11-09: Hevy's most-raised request, which it calls "genuinely tricky" to add under logged history; `src/lib/sides.ts`). A set's `weight` is the load of **one** dumbbell or kettlebell, and the whole load for barbells and machines. Each set records `implements` (1 or 2, from `implementsFor`) when it's logged, so a later change to the exercise list never rewrites history; older and imported sets without it fall back to the exercise (dumbbell exercises use a pair unless listed in `ONE_DUMBBELL`, kettlebell exercises use one bell). Per-side exercises (`PER_SIDE`: one-arm rows, split squats, lunges, single-leg calf raises) log reps for each side: both the same is the one-tap default, and an optional "Sides differ?" link under the reps adds a stepper for the right side (`Active.repsR`), so a set can carry `repsL` and `repsR`, with `reps` as the weaker side for progression. **Volume** everywhere (`setVolume`: sessions, totals, recaps, seasonal challenges, tonnage) is weight × implements × reps on every side, so two 20 kg dumbbells for 10 count 400 kg; volume figures from before this change went up for dumbbell work (no real users yet). The weight label says "each" only for pairs. e1RM, PRs and plate math stay per implement. Strong and Hevy log a dumbbell's own weight, which is what the importer assumes. **Showing it** (Melvin, 2026-11-12: "make it clear that certain exercises require you to do one side and then the other"): wherever a planned or logged set is written out (`repsText`/`setText` in `format.ts`: Today, Coming up, the rest card, the lock-screen notification and Live Activity, the Wear OS, Apple Watch and Garmin apps, history, friends' posts) a per-side move says "8 reps each side" or "30 s each side", and a set whose sides differed shows "10 left · 8 right"; the workout screen's reps stepper says "reps each side" or "seconds each side"; under the exercise name on the workout screen, in its how-to sheet and on the exercise page a line with the `sides` icon says "Reps are per side. Do them on both sides." ("Time is per side. Hold on both sides." for holds; `SideHint.tsx`), worded so it also fits alternating moves like walking lunges; and the exercise browser adds "Each side" to the row. Nothing is added during a set. Rep counters (camera, phone motion, watch) see both sides, so a per-side move's count is halved, rounded up (`countedReps`). The same day ~55 common one-side moves that had been missed were added to `PER_SIDE` (split squat, reverse and lateral lunges, step-ups, pistol, single-leg RDL and bridge, side plank and Copenhagen plank, bird dog, Pallof press, woodchops, Turkish get-up, windmill, archer push-up and pull-up, concentration curl, one-arm cable row, single-leg press, …) and the one-dumbbell ones to `ONE_DUMBBELL`; a test (`sides.test.ts`) fails when a move whose name says it's one-sided isn't flagged. **Carries are not per side** (Melvin, 2026-11-12: "dumbbell suitcase carry and most if not all carries are time based not side based, don't say switch sides"): suitcase, mixed and cross-body carries, the suitcase march and the one-sided dumbbell hold are one timed set with no "each side" and no switch in the set timer; a test keeps them out of `PER_SIDE`. Volume for those moves' earlier sets doubles (reps now count on both sides). **Per hand** (Melvin, 2026-11-12: "make it clear especially for dumbbell exercises that it's the weight per arm"): a pair's weight reads "20 kg per hand" everywhere (`fmt.each`, the stepper's "kg per hand"), and the workout screen, how-to sheet and exercise page add "Weight is per dumbbell, one in each hand." (`SetHints.tsx`); a one-dumbbell move's weight is just the dumbbell, with no "per hand". The phone and watch apps get it with no native code, because the native side shows the text the web layer sends.
- **First-session starting weights** (Melvin, 2026-11-09, after a 145 lb "experienced" lifter was offered a 175 lb squat). Each exercise's `start` is a share of body weight for about 10 reps. That gives an estimated one-rep max, scaled by experience (new 0.55, some 0.8, experienced 1.0, because self-reported experience runs high), sex (women 0.6 upper body and 0.75 lower; unknown sits between), and age (from 40, 0.92 down to 0.65 at 70+). The first session then aims 2 reps further from failure than the block asks (`FIRST_RIR`). Known numbers entered in setup (squat, bench, deadlift, overhead press as weight × reps) replace the estimate for that lift, and scale to close relatives at 85%. **Quick calibration:** if the first session shows the start was light ("too easy", 2+ reps over target, or the lifter raised the weight), the next session jumps to what the logged sets show, capped at +25%, instead of one step at a time. That 145 lb lifter now gets 120 lb × 6 (135 lb if they say male).
- **Variability.** Consistent keeps an exercise for a muscle about 75% of the time, Balanced 50%, Variable 25%.
- **Swaps.** Alternatives share primary muscles and must be doable at the gym, ranked by muscle overlap and movement pattern. A swap marked "not available here" keeps that exercise out of plans at that gym for 14 days.
- **Plates.** Every suggested barbell weight is one the lifter's plates can make. The plate solver is a bounded knapsack (a greedy fill misses some combinations), prefers fewest plates, then heaviest first, and works in the gym's unit so 45 lb plates stay exact (`plates.ts`). **Every plate size has its own colour** (Melvin, 2026-11-11: "every plate should be a different colour"; 45 lb and 2.5 lb used to share blue, and 20 kg and 1.25 kg too). The big plates use the competition colours (25 kg / 55 lb red, 20 kg / 45 lb blue, 15 kg / 35 lb yellow, 10 kg / 25 lb green, 5 kg / 10 lb white); the change plates follow the powerlifting habit of black (2.5 kg, 5 lb) and silver (1.25 kg, 2.5 lb) instead of repeating red and blue, with orange and purple for the rarer small ones (`PLATE_HUES` in `plates.ts`). The colours are tokens (`--plate-*` in `globals.css`), with a white plate a touch whiter and a darker edge on light screens; every plate has an edge (`--plate-edge`) so a white plate shows on a light card and a black one on a dark card. `a11y.test.ts` checks, in both themes, that each lb and kg set has no two plates alike, that the number on each plate is 4.5:1, and that the edge is 3:1 against the card. Shared web code, so Android, iOS and the web get it together.
- **Units.** Weights are stored in kilograms; a gym's plates, bars and dumbbells are stored in the gym's unit and converted at the edges (`units.ts`).
- **Every exercise draws its own gear and variation in the demo** (Melvin, 2026-11-12: swapping an exercise "doesn't seem to change the 3D animation to match", "different exercises with the same animations"). The 1,000 moves share a few dozen pose families, so 637 of the swap sheet's top-six pairs drew exactly the same thing. Two layers on top of `MOTIONS`, applied in `motionFor`: `equipmentMotion.ts` reads the exercise's `needs` and draws what it holds (dumbbells, a kettlebell, a medicine ball (a new `ball` load in both the 3D and 2D scenes), a bar racked on the back for barbell versions of bodyweight lunges and squats, nothing for bodyweight moves) and what's around it (bands from the feet, no handle bar on band moves, ring straps from overhead, Smith machine rails either side of the bar); `variantMotion.ts` reads the id: `-hold` moves hold still at the hard point, negatives lower over four seconds with a quick reset, and alternating moves work one arm or leg at a time. Tempo and paused lifts play their tempo (`tempoMotion.ts`, [Tempo coach](tempo-coach.md)). `swapLooks.test.ts` counts the look-alike pairs left as a ratchet (206 since 2026-11-14, was 293: mostly true twins such as steady and interval cardio or jump rope styles, plus close cousins still sharing a pose); lower it as moves get their own motions, never raise it. Shared web code, so Android, iOS and the web get it together.
- **Planks and crawls that move get their own motions** (Melvin, 2026-11-13: the sideways plank walk "is wrong"; it played the forearm plank holding still). `motions.ts` has two four-point bases, a straight-armed high plank (`highPlankBase`) and the bear position with the knees hovering (`bearBase`), plus `stepper`, which moves hands and feet in turn (each step its own phase, the moving hand or foot lifting in a small arc, the body following by half a step and the travel centred so the camera frames it). The sideways plank walk is a high plank where the hand and foot on one side step out together and the others follow, hips level, two steps right and two back; the sideways bear crawl does the same in the bear position, and the backward bear crawl steps opposite hand and foot back two strides and returns. Also given their own motions the same day, because they borrowed a different move: plank jacks (feet jump wide and in, they played mountain climbers), high plank (on the hands, it showed the forearm plank), bear plank (a still hold, it crawled), plank and bear plank shoulder taps, plank up-downs (forearms to hands one arm at a time), plank leg lifts, the plank saw and the side plank hip drop. `swapLooks.test.ts` checks each one moves the way its name says. Shared web code, so Android, iOS and the web get the same animation.
