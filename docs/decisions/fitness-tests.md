# Fitness tests (Melvin asked, 2026-11-11: "there is apparently robust research that uses how long someone can hang from a pull up bar to measure a person's overall health … add this functionality to the app"; built)

Part of the decision record. [AGENTS.md](../../AGENTS.md) has the rules that apply to every change and the list of topics. Record decisions about this topic here, in the same change as the code.

## What the research says (checked 2026-11-11)

- **The dead hang has no health research of its own.** No study ties hang time to health outcomes, and there are no published adult norms by age and sex; the age charts online are coaches' targets or a site's own formula (they say so). The only measured adult numbers we found: Stepan et al., *Applied Sciences* 2026;16(19):9926 (doi 10.3390/app16199926), 38 adults aged 24 to 62, overhand grip, bare hands: men 77.7 ± 37.4 s, women 44.4 ± 23.2 s, all 61.1 ± 35.0 s. Hang time there follows grip strength relative to body weight (R² 0.44).
- **The robust research is grip strength.** PURE (Leong et al., *The Lancet* 2015;386:266-273, 139,691 adults in 17 countries): each 5 kg lower grip went with a 16% higher hazard of death from any cause. That's a dynamometer, not a hang, so the app calls the hang "a grip and shoulder check you can track" and cites PURE only as why grip is worth tracking, never as a claim about the hang.
- **One-leg stand:** Springer et al., *J Geriatr Phys Ther* 2007;30(1), Table 1: 549 healthy adults aged 18 to 99, barefoot, arms crossed, stopped at 45 s, best of three, eyes open and closed, mean and SD by sex and six age groups (`BALANCE_REF`).
- **30-second chair stand:** Lein et al., *Int J Sports Phys Ther* 2022;17(5):907-914: 81 adults aged 18 to 35, 45 cm chair, 33.0 ± 5.4 (no sex split). Rikli and Jones, *J Aging Phys Act* 1999;7(2):162-181: normal ranges (middle half) by sex and five-year group, ages 60 to 94; their lower ends are the same as CDC STEADI's below-average cut-offs. **Ages 36 to 59 have no reference we could check**, so they compare with their own results.
- **Left out:** wall sit, box step-ups and farmer's carry (from Melvin's screenshot) have no peer-reviewed norms we could find, so the app doesn't show made-up percentiles for them. The sitting-rising test (Brito et al., *Eur J Prev Cardiol* 2014) has strong research, but its reference tables (Araújo et al. 2020) weren't reachable to check; a candidate for later.

## What's built

- **A "Fitness tests" card on Body › Health**, under Fitness age (`FitTestsCard` in `FitTests.tsx`, engine `src/lib/fitTests.ts`, text `ft.*`). Three rows (dead hang: grip and shoulders; one-leg stand: balance; chair stand: leg strength), each the one button to its sheet, showing the last result and "Retest in N days" or "Retest due" (every 4 weeks, `RETEST_DAYS`; fitness changes slowly), or "Take the test".
- **Each sheet:** three steps; for the one-leg stand an Eyes open / Eyes closed chip; a timer (5-second get-ready with ticks, then the hang and one-leg stand count up until Stop, the one-leg stand stops itself at 45 s, the chair stand counts down from 30; the rest-over chime at the end; the screen stays on); a − / + box the timer fills (a friend's stopwatch works too; up to three one-leg tries, best kept); Save result.
- **Result:** the number, a chip (Above typical, Typical, Room to build, or Full 45 seconds; never red or amber), last, best and change, the typical range with its source in words, what helps (one line per test), and a goal for next time (hang: best + 10 s rounded to 5; chair: best + 2; one-leg: best + 5 s up to 45, and once at 45 s, "try it with your eyes closed").
- **Typical** is within half an SD of a study's mean, or the published middle half. No percentiles: the studies are small or give only mean and SD, so a percentile would claim more precision than they have. The hang compares only with the one small study and says so; outside ages 18 to 65 it doesn't compare.
- **Data:** `fitTest` events (`test`, `value`: seconds or stands, `eyes` for the one-leg stand), so they sync, back up and stay on the device like other health data (never analytics, feedback or share links). Free, like the rest of the twin.
- **Wellness rules:** the same as [Fitness age](fitness-age.md): it's called "Fitness tests", not "Longevity tests" (a lifespan claim), no disease, death or "live longer" words (`FitTests.test.tsx` checks), never a notification, always the source and what to do, and the general-wellness note on the card and in every sheet, which also says to stop if anything hurts.
- **Fitness age doesn't use these results** (2026-11-11). Fitness age is VO₂ max against HUNT's reference; none of these tests has a validated age conversion, and mixing them would make the number less trustworthy. They sit side by side on Health instead.
- Pure web code, so Android, iOS and the web app get it together with no native code. The a11y run scans the dead hang's sheet.
