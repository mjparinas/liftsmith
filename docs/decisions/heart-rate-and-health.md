# Heart rate and health

Part of the decision record. [AGENTS.md](../../AGENTS.md) has the rules that apply to every change and the list of topics. Record decisions about this topic here, in the same change as the code.

- **Live heart rate comes over Bluetooth**, not Health Connect, because Health Connect gets Fitbit data in batches, often minutes late. Any standard heart rate broadcaster works (Heart Rate Service 0x180D): Fitbit Charge 6 and Pixel Watch "share heart rate", Polar, Garmin, Wahoo straps (`heartRate.ts`).
- **Health Connect** (Android) and **Apple Health** (iOS) go through one plugin, `@capgo/capacitor-health` (`src/lib/health.ts`). Reads (weight, height, body fat, resting HR, plus sleep, HRV, steps, VO2 max and respiratory rate for the health twin; nothing else, see "Google Play review" below) prefill the wizard's body weight step ("Fill in from Health Connect / Apple Health"). Finished workouts are written as active calories (about 5 METs) and average HR, because the plugin can't write workout sessions yet. The Android Health Connect privacy rationale is `public/privacypolicy.html`.
- **Connecting never fails silently or hangs** (Melvin, 2026-11-09, after a Fitbit Air showed in Chrome's picker and then nothing happened). `connectHeartRate` times out each step (10 s connect, 8 s service, 30 s for a pairing prompt), retries the GATT connect up to three times (Chrome's first connect to a watch often fails or never answers), and throws an `HrError` whose `problem` is `cancelled`, `bluetoothOff`, `blocked`, `unreachable` (in use by another app or machine, or out of range), `noService` (no standard 0x180D service), `pairing` or `failed`. The workout's Connect button and the setup tile show "Connecting…" and then `hrMessage`'s plain-words reason (a closed picker shows nothing); for a Fitbit or Pixel Watch the message adds how to turn sharing on (Fitbit Air, Edge, Charge 6: Google Health app › Connections › the device › Share heart rate › Get started; Pixel Watch: Connected Fitness in quick settings). If notifications fail on Android and the device isn't bonded, the app asks Android to pair (`createBond`) and tries again; iOS and browsers show their own pairing prompt when the watch asks. Same code on Android, iOS and the web.
- **The monitor reconnects by itself** (Melvin, 2026-11-10, after his Fitbit Air couldn't be found again for a second workout until he turned sharing off and on in the Google Health app). A watch that shares heart rate through its companion app stays linked to the phone and stops advertising, so a fresh scan can't see it. So:
  - *Remembered:* the last monitor that connected is saved on the device (`liftsmith.hr.device` in localStorage: its id and name; the setup step's test connect counts).
  - *No scan first:* Connect tries that monitor by id before any picker (Android connects by address; iOS looks it up with `getDevices`, which it needs before connecting). If its address changed, a bonded (Android) or connected (iOS: connected heart rate devices) device with the same name is tried next. Only then does the picker open.
  - *Automatic:* when a workout starts, a remembered monitor connects without a tap, once and quietly (no picker, no error); the Connect button stays for the lifter.
  - *Drops:* if the link drops mid-workout, the app reconnects on its own after 2, 4, 8 and 15 s, then every 30 s, about 3 minutes in all (`RETRY_MS` in `hrLink.ts`); the button says "Reconnecting…" (tap to try now), and after the last try the lifter is told the monitor was lost.
  - *Released cleanly:* however a workout ends (End, Discard, the automatic end of a forgotten workout), readings are stopped and then the link is dropped, so the watch is free for the next workout or another app.
  - *When nothing is found:* Android's empty scan is its own message ("No heart rate monitor showed up…") instead of a generic failure; a Fitbit that connected before gets the fix that works (sharing off and on in the Google Health app), and with no monitor known the Fitbit sharing steps are added.
  - The link lives in `src/lib/hrLink.ts` (`hrLink`), outside the workout screen, so leaving for Today and coming back keeps the monitor. Same code on Android and iOS through the BLE plugin, no new native code; the web build reconnects without the picker only in browsers that keep Bluetooth permission (`navigator.bluetooth.getDevices`, Chrome), otherwise the picker opens as before.
- Native heart rate uses `@capacitor-community/bluetooth-le` on both platforms (keeps reading with the screen off; iOS has the `bluetooth-central` background mode). The web build keeps Web Bluetooth.
- **The live heart rate is red** (Melvin, 2026-11-10, testing on his phone: "the heart rate component at the top of the screen should be red"). The workout header's bpm badge is red text, a red heart and a faint red edge (`--color-heart`: `#f0605d` dark, `#c4262b` light, both 5:1+ on the screen and cards); it used to change colour with the heart rate zone. It stays red with colour-blind safe colours, because it's the heart's colour, not a status, and the heart icon and the number carry the meaning. The Connect button's heart uses the same red.
- **A trend line beside the bpm on the 3D figure** (Melvin, 2026-11-10: "small and minimalistic but stylish"). The figure's stage shows the red heart, the bpm and a 44 × 14 line of the last two minutes of readings (`src/lib/hrTrend.ts`, drawn by `HeartReadout.tsx`), fading in from the left and ending in a soft dot at the latest reading. It's scaled to at least a 12 bpm span so a steady heart rate draws a calm line, and needs two readings before it shows. It reads the workout's own heart rate stream (`active.hr`), so it's the same on Android, iOS and the web. The stage is always dark, so it uses the dark red (`DEMO_COLOURS.heart`, kept equal to the token by `a11y.test.ts`).
- **Every heart keeps time with the live bpm** (Melvin asked, 2026-11-10: "is the heart beating in sync with my heart rate"). The heart on the figure's chest (3D and the 2D fallback) beats at the live bpm from the monitor or the watch (`pickBpm`); its phase is carried from frame to frame (`advanceBeat` in `engine.ts`), so when the reading changes it speeds up or slows down from where it is instead of jumping and skipping a beat. The small heart icons beside the bpm (on the figure's stage and in the workout header) pulse at the same rate (`beatStyle` in `src/lib/beat.ts`, clamped to 30 to 240 bpm) rather than CSS's fixed once a second. It follows the rate, not each real beat: Bluetooth heart rate sends a reading about once a second, not the moment of every beat. No monitor, no heart. Web code, so Android, iOS and the web match.
